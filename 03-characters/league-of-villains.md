# Villains

*The League of Villains, the Meta Liberation Army, the Paranormal Liberation Front, and independent antagonists.*

> **Content note:** This page contains spoilers for events beyond Season 5, including the final arcs of the series. Later developments are marked ⚠️.

---

## 1. The League of Villains

Founded by All For One and led by Tomura Shigaraki. Originally a small criminal gang, it becomes — after merging with the Meta Liberation Army — the **Paranormal Liberation Front**, an organisation of over one hundred thousand members.

---

### Tomura Shigaraki (死柄木 弔) — *born Tenko Shimura (志村 転弧)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Decay; later All For One |
| **Birthday** | April 4 |
| **Height** | 175 cm |
| **Role** | Leader, League of Villains; Supreme Leader, Paranormal Liberation Front |
| **Voice (JP)** | Koki Uchiyama |
| **Voice (EN)** | Eric Vale |
| **Debut** | Season 1, episode 10 — the U.S.J. attack |

The series' primary antagonist — and its most tragic character.

**Origin.** Tenko Shimura was the grandson of **Nana Shimura**, the seventh wielder of One For All and All Might's mentor. Terrified that All For One would target her bloodline, Nana sent her son **Kotaro** away and cut off all contact, asking that he be told she was dead. Kotaro grew up hating her, and hating heroes. He married a woman named Hana, had Tenko, and forbade the boy from becoming a hero — beating him for it, and refusing to acknowledge that Tenko had a Quirk at all.

One day, Decay awakened. Tenko decayed his dog. Then his mother. Then his two sisters. Then his father. Then his entire neighbourhood. He was about five years old.

He wandered the streets afterwards, scratching at his neck until it bled, because the scratching was the only thing that made the feeling stop. He asked for help. **No one came.**

**All For One found him.** He gave the boy a new name — Tomura Shigaraki, from 弔 (*tomurai*), "to mourn" — deliberately keeping the sound of *Shimura* so the connection would never quite be severed. He raised him on hatred.

⚠️ The final chapters reveal that this was not opportunism. All For One had engineered the circumstances of Tenko's life — the family, the neglect, the awakening of the Quirk — as part of a plan that predates the boy's birth.

**Quirk.** Decay disintegrates anything Shigaraki touches with all five fingers of one hand, spreading through connected matter. Initially it required full five-finger contact on a single object. After his awakening in the Meta Liberation Army arc it spreads outward from anything he touches, in a chain reaction he cannot stop — he can destroy a city block by touching the ground.

The neck-scratching is a self-soothing behaviour from childhood that eventually draws blood. It disappears once he awakens.

**Character.** Shigaraki begins as petulant and childish: he calls people "trash," throws tantrums, and disintegrates subordinates who annoy him. Over the series he becomes something far worse — calm, purposeful, and ideologically coherent. His stated goal is not conquest but **the destruction of hero society itself**, because he believes a world that produces people like him cannot be saved, only levelled.

The devastating irony of the series' structure: **All Might's mentor's grandson is the villain All Might spent his life fighting.** One For All and All For One have been warring across generations through a single family, and Nana Shimura's attempt to protect her bloodline is what destroyed it.

### All For One

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | All For One |
| **Age** | over 100 |
| **Role** | Founder and true leader of the League of Villains |
| **Voice (JP)** | Akio Otsuka |
| **Voice (EN)** | John Swasey |
| **Debut** | Season 1 (referenced); in person, Season 3 episode 47 |

The oldest and most powerful villain in the series. All For One can **steal any Quirk from another person** and **transfer Quirks to others**. He has lived well over a century by accumulating life-extending and healing Quirks, and he wields dozens of stolen abilities simultaneously.

He is the direct mirror of One For All: where One For All is power freely given across generations, All For One is power taken from others and hoarded. He treats people as resources and Quirks as inventory.

**History.** In the era when Quirks first appeared, All For One ruled Japan's underworld. His younger brother, **Yoichi Shigaraki**, appeared to be Quirkless — but All For One had *given* him a stockpiling Quirk, as an act of contempt. Yoichi combined that stolen power with his own latent will and passed it on, creating the one Quirk All For One could never steal back. Every subsequent wielder has been fighting the same man.

All For One wounded All Might catastrophically in their first battle, destroying most of his stomach and a lung. That single injury is why All Might has a time limit, and therefore why the entire series happens.

**Kamino Ward (Season 3).** He reveals to All Might that he killed **Nana Shimura**. All Might defeats him with United States of Smash and he is imprisoned in **Tartarus**. He is *smiling* as he is taken away, because the Symbol of Peace has fallen in front of the whole country — which was the objective.

⚠️ **Later.** He escapes, recovers Shigaraki, and ultimately transfers his own Quirk into him, intending to overwrite Shigaraki's personality and use his body as a vessel. Whether this succeeds is one of the central questions of the series' final arcs.

### Toya Todoroki (轟 燈矢) — *Dabi*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Blueflame (Cremation) |
| **Birthday** | January 18 |
| **Height** | 176 cm |
| **Age** | 23–24 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Hiro Shimono |
| **Voice (EN)** | Jason Liebrecht |
| **Debut** | Season 3, episode 44 |

Blue flames far hotter than Endeavor's. His body cannot withstand them: Toya inherited his father's fire Quirk but his **mother's tolerance for cold rather than his father's tolerance for heat**, so using his Quirk burns him. The purple staples and skin grafts across his body are the accumulated result.

**History.** Toya trained desperately as a child to earn Endeavor's approval. When his body began to fail him, Endeavor declared him insufficient and turned his attention to Shoto instead. Toya kept training in secret anyway, and one day burned himself almost to death on a mountain. He threw himself into a lake to stop the flames.

He survived. He was comatose for three years, and was rebuilt in a facility run by **All For One and Doctor Garaki** — who intended to groom him as a subordinate. He woke to find his voice changed and his body mostly replaced. He refused to be trained by anyone but his father, set the facility on fire, and went home.

His family had already mourned him and moved on. Endeavor had begun abusing Shoto in exactly the same way. Toya accepted his own "death," dyed his hair black, and rebuilt himself as **Dabi** — with the single purpose of destroying Endeavor's reputation rather than his body.

⚠️ **The reveal.** Dabi reveals himself as Toya Todoroki in the **Paranormal Liberation War arc** — manga chapter 290, Season 6, in the episode titled *"Dabi's Dance."* He rides into the battlefield on Gigantomachia's back, washes the dye out of his hair in front of Endeavor and Shoto, and simultaneously broadcasts a pre-recorded confession to every screen in the country. In it he details Endeavor's Quirk marriage, the abuse, and his own supposed death, and confirms his identity with a DNA test performed on Endeavor's blood left over from the Jaku City fight — a 99.99% match.

He also reveals that he **arranged Endeavor's encounters with Hood, Starservant and Ending**, engineering the No. 1 Hero's rise specifically so that the fall would be worth watching.

The broadcast does more damage to hero society than any battle in the series. It is not a fight; it is a press conference.

**Character.** Dabi is nihilistic and cruel, but his cruelty is legible: he wants his father to feel what he felt. Earlier in the war, when Hawks asks him who he really is, Dabi answers honestly — and Hawks' reaction makes clear the answer was explosive, long before the public learns it.

### Himiko Toga (渡我 被子)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Transform |
| **Birthday** | August 7 |
| **Height** | 157 cm |
| **Voice (JP)** | Misato Fukuen |
| **Voice (EN)** | Leah Clark |
| **Debut** | Season 3, episode 44 |

Drinks a person's blood and assumes their appearance — and, later in the series, can use their Quirk while transformed. She fights with knives and is dangerously fast at close range.

**Origin.** Toga was a normal girl whose Quirk made her find blood beautiful. When she showed her parents, they were horrified and told her to suppress it. She was put into therapy and instructed to become someone else — to smile normally, to think normally, to want normal things. She eventually broke, killed a boy she loved, and fled.

She is the series' most explicit argument that **hero society manufactures its villains**. Nothing was wrong with Toga except that no one would let her be what she was.

**Character.** She is cheerful, affectionate, and completely without boundaries. She loves people by wanting to *become* them — she is obsessed with both Midoriya and Uraraka, and considers them friends. She joins the League's Vanguard Action Squad at the Forest Training Camp specifically because she finds the students appealing.

⚠️ Her final arc, in which she is confronted by Uraraka rather than fought, is the series' clearest statement that some villains should have been helped rather than defeated.

### Jin Bubaigawara (分倍河原 仁) — *Twice*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Double |
| **Birthday** | May 10 |
| **Height** | 178 cm |
| **Age** | 31 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Daichi Endo |
| **Voice (EN)** | Newton Pittman |
| **Debut** | Season 3, episode 44 |

Creates perfect duplicates of anything he can visualise and measure — objects, people, himself. Copies of living things decay after a period, but **copies of Twice can create further copies**, allowing exponential multiplication. He has a hard limit of two active clones of anything at a time, which is why he must measure precisely.

**Origin.** Twice formed a criminal gang. His comrades turned on each other, and he created copies of himself to survive — only to discover afterwards that he could no longer tell which one was the original. He killed them all, including possibly himself. He is left permanently unsure whether he is the real Jin Bubaigawara.

**Character.** He wears a full-body suit and mask, speaks in contradictory double-talk — *"I love you all"* immediately followed by *"get away from me"* — and functions as the League's comic relief. He is also its emotional heart. His devotion to Toga, Dabi and Compress is the only thing that stabilises him.

⚠️ In the Meta Liberation Army arc he produces **"Sad Man's Parade,"** an army of thousands of clones covering an entire city district, and single-handedly turns the battle against the Army. It is the most powerful demonstration of Double in the series, and it is precisely why he has to die.

⚠️ **His death.** During the Paranormal Liberation War, **Hawks** kills him while undercover — because Double is too dangerous to leave alive and because Twice was moments away from warning the Front of the raid. Dabi arrives in time to see him die. Twice dies happy, believing he had finally found real friends. It is the most morally complicated killing in the series.

### Atsuhiro Sako (迫 圧紘) — *Mr. Compress*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Compress |
| **Birthday** | October 8 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Tsuguo Mogami |
| **Voice (EN)** | Kent Williams |

Compresses anything within a circular area around his arm into a small marble, releasing it at will. He can compress people, buildings or entire landscapes, and carries the marbles in his coat.

A showman descended from the famous thief **Oji Harima**. Mr. Compress treats villainy as performance and is the League's most composed member. He captures Bakugo during the Forest Training Camp by compressing him into a marble and hiding it in his mouth — the single most effective act of the entire attack.

⚠️ In the Meta Liberation Army arc he drops the act entirely, revealing that the magician persona is a construction built over genuine grief. He loses an arm during the confrontation with Overhaul's transport and later receives a prosthetic.

### Shuichi Iguchi (伊口 秀一) — *Spinner*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Gecko |
| **Voice (JP)** | Ryo Iwasaki |
| **Voice (EN)** | Larry Brantley |

A lizard mutation-type Quirk allowing him to cling to surfaces. He wields a large sword composed of many smaller blades bound together.

Spinner joined the League because he admired **Stain**. He is a mutant-type — the category most openly discriminated against in hero society — and Stain's critique explained his own life to him. He is the League's most ideologically motivated member, and the character who most directly connects Stain's philosophy to Shigaraki's.

⚠️ As the series progresses he becomes increasingly radical, and by the final arcs he is one of the Front's principal field commanders, leading the mutants who have the least reason to want the society they are attacking to survive.

### Kurogiri (黒霧) — *Warp Gate*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Warp Gate |
| **Voice (JP)** | Takahiro Fujiwara |
| **Voice (EN)** | Chuck Huber |

Creates teleportation portals from the dark mist that composes his body. He is the League's transport, its barman, and its de facto caretaker of Shigaraki — the only adult presence in the group, and the reason the League can operate at all.

**The truth.** Kurogiri is a **Nomu** constructed by Doctor Garaki from the corpse of **Oboro Shirakumo** — a U.A. student and close friend of Aizawa and Present Mic, who died young. His residual personality surfaces in Season 5, episode 107, when Aizawa and Yamada interrogate him in Tartarus and Yamada calls him by Shirakumo's name.

⚠️ The discovery becomes a major thread in Season 6. Kurogiri is also the reason the U.S.J. attack and the Forest Training Camp attack were possible at all — and the fact that he is made of the heroes' dead friend is the cruelest structural joke in the series.

### Doctor Kyudai Garaki (殻木 久大) — *alias Daruma Ujiko*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Life Force |
| **Age** | 120+ |
| **Voice (JP)** | Minoru Inaba |
| **Voice (EN)** | Mark Stoddard |

All For One's physician and the creator of the **Nomu**. His Quirk doubles his vitality, which is why he has lived for over a century. He operates under numerous false identities — including a respected public doctor and a hospital director — and runs the laboratory beneath **Jaku General Hospital** that produces the Nomu and High-End Nomu.

He is responsible for Kurogiri, for the reconstruction of Toya Todoroki, for the research done on Eri's Rewind, and for the escalation of Nomu technology that makes the Paranormal Liberation War possible. He is also the character who tells Dabi, flatly, that his body was only expected to last another month — and that his grudge kept him alive.

### Gigantomachia

| | |
|---|---|
| **Height** | approximately 25 m |
| **Voice (JP)** | Yasuhiro Mamiya |
| **Voice (EN)** | Cris George |

A colossal, near-indestructible creature created by Garaki from multiple combined Quirks, and fanatically loyal to All For One. He sleeps for most of the series, waking only when All For One's successor appears.

⚠️ When Shigaraki awakens during the Paranormal Liberation War, Gigantomachia crosses Japan on foot to reach him, destroying everything in his path. He cannot be stopped by conventional means, and the heroes' entire strategy becomes one of redirecting him rather than defeating him. Even after Shigaraki is possessed by All For One's vestige, Gigantomachia keeps fighting — as revenge for being abandoned.

## 2. Independent and Affiliated Villains

### Chizome Akaguro (赤黒 血染) — *Stain, the Hero Killer*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Bloodcurdle |
| **Birthday** | June 14 |
| **Age** | 31 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Go Inoue |
| **Voice (EN)** | Robert McCollum |

Paralyses anyone whose blood he ingests, for a duration proportional to the victim's blood type. A serial killer who murdered or crippled numerous Pro Heroes.

Stain is the series' first ideological antagonist. His thesis: modern heroes are celebrities motivated by fame and money, and only those willing to sacrifice themselves without reward are legitimate. He was once an admirer of All Might who became convinced that everyone who followed him had missed the point entirely.

He crippled the Pro Hero **Ingenium** — Tenya Iida's older brother — permanently, and kills the Pro Hero **Native** in Hosu City before being stopped. He is defeated by Midoriya, Todoroki and Iida working together, and he **spares Midoriya**, because he judges his motive pure.

The narrative does not refute him entirely. Many Pro Heroes *are* motivated by money — Mt. Lady says so on camera in episode 1. Stain's manifesto spreads after his arrest and radicalises **Spinner** directly, and much of the subsequent villain population indirectly. The authorities cover up the Hosu Incident and credit Endeavor, which means the state's first response to a coherent critique of heroism is to lie about it.

### Kai Chisaki — *Overhaul*

| **Birthday** | March 20 |
| **Age** | 27 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Kenjiro Tsuda |

Disassembles and reassembles anything he touches, including human bodies. He can heal himself by reassembling his own tissue, kill by disassembling a person on contact, and merge himself with his surroundings to become a vastly larger form.

Leader of the young guard of the **Shie Hassaikai** yakuza group. He has severe mysophobia and wears gloves and a plague-doctor mask constantly. He believes Quirks are a disease that must be cured, and intends to sell the cure.

**The Eight Bullets** are his inner circle:

|---|---|---|
| **Shin Nemoto** | Confession | Forces anyone to answer questions truthfully; the group's strategist |
| **Rikiya Katsukame** | Energy Suck | Absorbs opponents' stamina and vitality |

Working above them are **Chronostasis** (Hari Kurono), whose clock-hand hair slows whatever it strikes, and **Mimic** (Joi Irinaka), who merges into walls and controls the compound's labyrinth.

**The abuse of Eri.** Overhaul discovered that **Eri's Rewind Quirk** could reverse a body to a prior state. He told her she was cursed, that her existence hurt people, and that she should not have been born.

He is defeated by Midoriya, who fights at **100% Full Cowl sustained indefinitely** — Eri's Rewind, triggered by his blood, undoes his injuries as fast as he inflicts them. Afterwards ⚠️ **Shigaraki destroys both his arms** during the League's interception of his prison transport, which — for a man whose Quirk requires touch — is a fate worse than death.

### Gentle Criminal (Danjuro Tobita) & La Brava (Manami Aiba)

| | Gentle | La Brava |
|---|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Elasticity | Love |
| **Birthday** | August 29 | February 14 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Koichi Yamadera | Yui Horie |
| **Voice (EN)** | John Gremillion | Megan Shipman |

A pair of small-time villains who appear in the U.A. School Festival arc. **Gentle** failed the U.A. entrance exam, lost his job and his family, and became a criminal purely for attention — he films his crimes and posts them online, where almost nobody watches. His Quirk lets him make air elastic, which he uses to bounce and to create platforms.

**La Brava** loves him, and her Quirk grants a one-time massive power boost to the person she loves most.

They are the series' gentlest villains. Midoriya defeats Gentle not by overpowering him but by understanding him — Midoriya was, until recently, exactly the same kind of person: someone with a dream that everyone around him said was impossible. Gentle surrenders, and both are arrested.

The arc exists to argue that hero society discards people who fail once, and that some villains are simply people who were never given a second chance.

### Kaina Tsutsumi (堤 河奈) — *Lady Nagant*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Rifle |
| **Birthday** | October 10 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Atsumi Tanezaki |
| **Voice (EN)** | Erica Lindbeck |

Her right arm transforms into a rifle, and she can curve her own bullets in flight with pinpoint accuracy. ⚠️ Formerly one of Japan's top-ranked Pro Heroes, and secretly an assassin for the **Hero Public Safety Commission**.

She was ordered to kill people the Commission considered threats — including heroes. She broke under the moral weight, murdered the Commission's chairman, and was imprisoned in Tartarus. All For One frees her and gives her the Air Walk Quirk, using her as a long-range weapon against Midoriya.

Lady Nagant is the series' clearest indictment of the Commission itself: an institution that maintains peace by committing atrocities quietly, and then discards the people who carried them out. She is also the character who articulates the series' central doubt out loud — that a peace built on hidden violence is not peace, but a delayed collapse.

## 3. The Meta Liberation Army

A secret organisation of over **100,000 members** who believe Quirks should be used freely, without government regulation. Founded historically by **Destro**; rebuilt in the modern era by his descendant.

The Army deliberately uses the retro terms "**Meta Abilities**" and "**superpowers**" rather than "Quirk," arguing that the modern word should not be used until liberation is achieved.

| Member | Real name | Position | Quirk | Voice (JP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Re-Destro** | Rikiya Yotsubashi | Supreme Commander; CEO of Detnerat | **Stress** — converts accumulated stress into size, strength and durability | Hiroaki Hirata |
| **Geten** | — | Commander | **Ice** — creates and manipulates ice | Seiichiro Yamashita |
| **Trumpet** | Koku Hanabata | Commander; leader of the Hearts and Mind Party | **Incite** — amplifies the physical and mental abilities of his followers | Junji Majima |
| **Skeptic** | Tomoyasu Chikazoku | Commander; board member of Feel Good Inc. | **Anthropomorph** — converts people into data; master hacker | Tomokazu Sugita |
| **Curious** | Chitose Kizuki | Commander; executive director of Shoowaysha Publishing | **Landmine** — anything she touches becomes explosive on subsequent contact | Takako Honda |
| **Slidin' Go** | Tatsuyuki Tokoname | Pro Hero secretly working for the Army | **Overclock** — accelerates his own movements | — |

The Army is not a criminal gang. It has a corporate front, a publishing arm, a political party and members embedded across ordinary Japanese life. Its existence is the season's argument that the League of Villains stopped being a threat the moment it acquired an ideology with institutional backing.

**Re-Destro vs. Shigaraki (Season 5, episodes 108–112).** The Army attempts to absorb or eliminate the League. Re-Destro beats Shigaraki comprehensively. In his darkest moment, Shigaraki has a vision of himself as Tenko Shimura and awakens Decay's true, unlimited form. He defeats Re-Destro.

Re-Destro — seeing in Shigaraki the perfect embodiment of liberation — **submits to him**. The Army merges with the League to form the **Paranormal Liberation Front**, with Shigaraki as Supreme Leader and the former Army executives as lieutenants.

## The Series' Argument About Villains

*My Hero Academia* is unusual in that nearly every major villain is a **product of hero society**:

- **Shigaraki** — a child who asked for help and received none
- **Dabi** — a son destroyed by his hero father's expectations
- **Toga** — a girl told her feelings were monstrous
- **Twice** — a man betrayed by people he trusted, left unsure whether he exists
- **Lady Nagant** — an agent broken by the institution she served
- **Gentle** — a man discarded for failing an exam
- **Aoyama's family** — Quirkless people who bought a power from All For One and were owned by him forever after
- **Stain** — a believer whose critique was correct enough to radicalise others
- **The Meta Liberation Army** — people who believe regulation itself is oppression

Horikoshi does not excuse any of them. They murder, torture and destroy. But he insists on showing the causal chain — and in doing so makes the argument that a society which only celebrates the strong will keep producing the people who tear it down.

The structural proof is the series' own premise. Midoriya and Shigaraki are the same kind of child: powerless, desperate for an adult to notice them. One was found by All Might on a rooftop. The other was found by All For One in a street. Everything that follows is the difference between those two afternoons.
