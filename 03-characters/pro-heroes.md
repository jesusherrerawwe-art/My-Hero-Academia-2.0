# Pro Heroes

*Licensed professional heroes operating outside U.A. High School.*

---

## The Hero Billboard Chart JP

Japan's official hero ranking, published twice a year and based on **resolved incidents** and **public approval ratings**. The two statistics do not always agree, and the series makes a point of it: after All Might's retirement, Endeavor has *lower* approval than both Best Jeanist and Hawks but ranks above them, because he resolves far more incidents.

This is the ranking that holds from chapter 184 through the rest of the series.

## Enji Todoroki (轟 炎司) — *Endeavor*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Hellflame |
| **Birthday** | August 8 |
| **Height** | 195 cm |
| **Rank** | No. 2 until Season 4; No. 1 thereafter |
| **Voice (JP)** | Tetsu Inada |
| **Voice (EN)** | Patrick Seitz |
| **Debut** | Season 2 |

The most complex adult character in the series. Endeavor generates and controls intense flames from his entire body. His techniques include **Flashfire Fist** — superheating his own body and releasing stored heat in a single burst — and his ultimate move, **Prominence Burn**, a focused pillar of flame hot enough to destroy a High-End Nomu's regeneration faster than it can rebuild.

**His sidekicks**, the Flaming Sidekickers, are **Burnin** (Moe Kamiji), **Onima** and **Kido**.

**The sin.** Endeavor spent his life obsessed with surpassing All Might. When he could not, he decided to **breed** someone who could. He arranged a marriage with **Rei**, a woman with an ice Quirk, specifically to produce a child with both fire and ice. He got **Shoto**.

He then trained Shoto relentlessly from age five, ignored his other three children — **Toya**, **Fuyumi** and **Natsuo** — and abused Rei to the point of a mental breakdown. She poured boiling water on Shoto's face. Endeavor had her committed.

Toya, who had inherited his father's fire but his mother's tolerance for cold rather than heat, burned himself every time he used his Quirk. Endeavor declared him too weak and stopped training him. **Toya was believed dead** after a fire on a mountain, and no remains were found.

**The redemption arc.** After All Might retires, Endeavor becomes No. 1 by default — a rank he did not earn and does not want. Rather than celebrate, he tells the country plainly that he is not All Might and never will be, and asks to be judged by what he does next.

In Season 5 he fights the High-End Nomu **Hood** in Jaku City alongside **Hawks** and his three work-study students — Midoriya, Bakugo and Shoto. He is nearly killed, and wins with Prominence Burn. Afterwards he tells the civilians, deliberately borrowing All Might's phrasing: *"It's fine now. Why? Because I am here."* He knows exactly what he is doing, and he knows he has not earned the sentence.

⚠️ **Spoiler.** Dabi reveals himself as Toya Todoroki in **Season 6**, during the Paranormal Liberation War (manga chapter 290) — not in Season 5. He had deliberately engineered Endeavor's encounters with Hood, Starservant and Ending specifically to set up the moment. His nationwide broadcast, in which he details Endeavor's abuse and confirms his identity with a DNA test performed on Endeavor's own blood from the Jaku fight, does more damage to public trust in hero society than any battle in the series.

**Why he matters.** Endeavor is the series' argument that redemption is not the same as forgiveness. He is never forgiven. Rei will not look at him; Natsuo refuses to speak to him; Shoto tolerates him at best. What he gets instead is **obligation** — the requirement to keep doing the right thing for the rest of his life, without relief. Horikoshi never resolves it, and that is deliberate.

## Keigo Takami (鷹見 啓悟) — *Hawks*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Fierce Wings |
| **Birthday** | December 28 |
| **Height** | 172 cm |
| **Age** | 22–23 |
| **Rank** | No. 3, then No. 2 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Yuichi Nakamura |
| **Voice (EN)** | Zeno Robinson |
| **Debut** | Season 4, episode 87 |

Red-feathered wings whose individual feathers he controls telekinetically. Each feather can be directed separately for reconnaissance, attack, restraint or rescue; he can regrow them, though it takes time and leaves him vulnerable. Each feather also carries sensory information back to him, which makes him an unmatched intelligence-gatherer. He is the fastest hero in Japan.

**Public persona.** Hawks is flippant, casual, and appears not to take anything seriously. He says he wants a world where heroes have too much free time to be needed. He reached the top ten at age 22, the youngest in history.

**The truth.** ⚠️ Hawks was recruited as a child by the **Hero Public Safety Commission**, which raised and trained him as an operative rather than as a hero. From Season 5 he works **undercover inside the Paranormal Liberation Front**, feeding intelligence to the Commission while befriending **Dabi** — who suspects him almost the entire time.

His undercover work requires him to do things a hero should not. He **kills Twice** — a villain he had genuinely come to like — because Double is too dangerous to leave alive and because Twice was moments away from warning the Front of the coming raid. Dabi arrives in time to see Twice die. The series does not soften this. Hawks is badly burned in the fight that follows and is left with wings that barely function.

**Character.** Hawks is the series' clearest critique of institutional heroism: a man raised to be a tool, who performs cheerfulness because no one ever taught him anything else. His wish that heroes should have free time is sincere — and it is also the wish of someone who has never had any.

## Tsunagu Hakamada (袴田 綱) — *Best Jeanist*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Fiber Master |
| **Birthday** | October 5 |
| **Rank** | No. 4, then No. 3 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Hikaru Midorikawa |
| **Voice (EN)** | Micah Solusod |

Controls the fibres in any fabric, allowing him to bind, immobilise and manipulate opponents through their clothing — including their own. He is precise, elegant, and obsessive about presentation; during Bakugo's work-study he personally corrects Bakugo's appearance, posture and speech, which Bakugo resents and then, slowly, absorbs.

Best Jeanist is critically injured by **All For One**, who attacks a group of top-ranked heroes to harvest their Quirks before the Kamino Ward battle and uses Jeanist's Fiber Master during the fight itself. Jeanist survives but spends the following months in a coma, and the public believes him dead.

He retains the No. 3 rank on the Season 4 chart despite being absent from the ceremony, and later recovers and returns to active duty.

## Shinya Kamihara (紙原 忍夜) — *Edgeshot*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Foldabody |
| **Birthday** | February 22 |
| **Rank** | No. 5, then No. 4 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Kenta Kamakari |
| **Voice (EN)** | John Burgmeier |

Can fold his body flat and thin — to a width of around half a millimetre — giving him extreme speed, the ability to slip into gaps no one else could enter, and a genuine cutting edge. A ninja-themed hero, and one of the most tactically intelligent Pro Heroes in the series.

Edgeshot has the third-highest approval rating in the top ten and says, at the chart ceremony, that he will work harder on Kamui Woods' behalf. He is one of the few top-ranked heroes who treats the ranking as a responsibility rather than a prize.

## Rumi Usagiyama (兎山 ルミ) — *Mirko*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Rabbit |
| **Birthday** | March 1 |
| **Height** | 159 cm |
| **Age** | 26 |
| **Rank** | No. 7, then No. 5 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Sayaka Kinoshita |
| **Voice (EN)** | Anairis Quiñones |

A rabbit mutation-type Quirk granting extraordinary leg strength, speed and jumping ability. Mirko fights **exclusively with her legs** and has no ranged options whatsoever — a deliberate design choice that reflects her personality.

She is the most aggressive of the top-ranked heroes: she operates solo, refuses sidekicks, and charges into situations that other heroes avoid. At the chart ceremony she issues a public warning to villains who are "rising up," which is the first official acknowledgment that the country's villain population is growing.

⚠️ During the Paranormal Liberation War she fights multiple High-End Nomu alone, loses an arm and takes catastrophic damage to her legs, and **keeps fighting**. She becomes one of the series' most popular characters precisely because she refuses to stop, and she continues to operate — with prosthetics — in the epilogue, ranked No. 6.

## Yu Takeyama (岳山 優) — *Mt. Lady*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Gigantification |
| **Birthday** | August 11 |
| **Rank** | outside the top ten until the epilogue |
| **Voice (JP)** | Kaori Nazuka |
| **Voice (EN)** | Jamie Marchi |

Grows to a fixed height of 20.6 metres — the Quirk has no intermediate sizes, which is a significant tactical limitation. Mt. Lady is frank about her motivations: she entered heroism for fame and money, and says so on camera in episode 1.

Her arc across the series is a slow, unglamorous shift toward genuine heroism. She becomes increasingly willing to take risks that do not benefit her public image, and her partnership with **Kamui Woods** and **Edgeshot** develops into real teamwork. By the war arcs she is one of the heroes the others rely on most, and in the epilogue she reaches **No. 3**.

She is the series' clearest example of Stain's critique being *correct* about some heroes — and then complicated by those heroes growing anyway.

## Shinji Nishiya (西宮 真二) — *Kamui Woods*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Arbor |
| **Birthday** | May 20 |
| **Rank** | No. 7 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Masamichi Kitada |
| **Voice (EN)** | Aaron Roberts |

Manipulates his body's wood-like composition, extending branches to restrain or strike. His signature technique, **Lacquer Chain Prison**, immobilises large groups instantly and is one of the most effective crowd-control abilities in the series. ## Taishiro Toyomitsu (豊満 太志郎) — *Fat Gum*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Fat Absorption |
| **Birthday** | August 8 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Kazuyuki Okitsu |
| **Voice (EN)** | Kyle Hebert |

His body absorbs kinetic impact, storing it and releasing it as a single devastating counterattack. The more damage he takes, the more powerful he becomes — at the cost of burning through his body mass, which leaves him visibly emaciated after a serious fight.

Fat Gum is cheerful, loud, and food-obsessed. He is also the mentor who takes **Eijiro Kirishima** and **Tamaki Amajiki** under his wing during the Shie Hassaikai raid, where his fight against **Kendo Rappa** and **Hekiji Tengai** is one of Season 4's best sequences. Rappa punches at machine-gun speed; Tengai shields him with barriers; Fat Gum absorbs everything and waits for the opening.

He is a genuinely excellent teacher. He recognises Kirishima's courage immediately and builds on it rather than correcting it, which is the opposite of what Best Jeanist does to Bakugo and the opposite of what Nighteye does to Midoriya.

## Mirai Sasaki (佐々木 未来) — *Sir Nighteye*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Foresight |
| **Birthday** | January 2 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Shin-ichiro Miki |
| **Voice (EN)** | Brandon McInnis |

All Might's former sidekick. Foresight lets him see exactly one hour of a person's future, once per day, and it **cannot be turned off** — everything he sees is guaranteed to happen.

Nighteye is humourless, exacting, and deeply devoted to All Might. Their friendship ended over a professional disagreement: Nighteye believed All Might should retire while he still could, and All Might refused. They did not speak for years.

He groomed **Mirio Togata** to inherit One For All, and considered Mirio objectively superior to Midoriya. When All Might chose Midoriya instead, Nighteye used Foresight on him and saw **Midoriya's death**. He spent the entire Shie Hassaikai arc trying to prevent it — which is the real reason he is so cold to Midoriya. He is not being cruel; he is watching a boy walk toward something he has already seen.

He is mortally wounded by Overhaul during the raid. As he dies he reconciles with All Might, telling him he should have smiled more, and his last wish is that Midoriya have a bright future. **Midoriya survives because of Eri's Rewind**, which Nighteye never learned about. Whether his vision was wrong, or whether it was fulfilled in a way he could not interpret, is left deliberately unresolved.

**His agency** also employs **Bubble Girl** (Kaoruko Awata) and **Centipeder** (Juzo Moashi) as sidekicks.

## Sorahiko Torino (鳥野 空彦) — *Gran Torino*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Jet |
| **Birthday** | January 28 |
| **Height** | 120 cm |
| **Voice (JP)** | Kenichi Ogata |
| **Voice (EN)** | Charles C. Campbell |

**All Might's teacher.** He propels himself with jets of air from his feet at speeds that make him nearly untouchable in close quarters. Small, elderly, and apparently senile, he is in fact the sharpest analyst in the series.

Gran Torino takes Midoriya on as a work-study student after the Sports Festival and teaches him the single most important lesson about One For All: that Midoriya has been treating it as a special move he fires from one limb, rather than as a part of his body. That insight produces **Full Cowl**.

He is also the character who most directly confronts All Might with his failures — including the fact that All Might never investigated what happened to Nana Shimura's family after her death, which is how her grandson ended up with All For One. Gran Torino blames himself as well: he was supposed to protect that family and did not.

## Kugo Sakamata (坂又 空吾) — *Gang Orca*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Orca |
| **Birthday** | October 29 |
| **Rank** | No. 10 before All Might's retirement |
| **Voice (JP)** | Shuhei Matsuda |
| **Voice (EN)** | Tyler Walker |

A killer-whale mutation hero who uses ultrasonic attacks and overwhelming physical strength. He is feared by students and villains alike for his intimidating appearance — which he leans into deliberately, and which he is openly irritated by.

He serves as an examiner in the second round of the Provisional Hero Licence Exam, where he hunts the examinees to test whether they can keep protecting people while being terrified. He then supervises **Bakugo and Todoroki's remedial course**, and delivers the season's clearest critique of both: Bakugo's contempt for others and Todoroki's emotional withdrawal are disqualifying flaws in a hero, not personality quirks.

## The Wild, Wild Pussycats

A four-person hero team specialising in mountain and forest rescue, introduced in Season 3 (manga chapter 70 / episode 40). Their agency is the **Pussycats Agency**, and each member's hero name — except Tiger's — comes from a domestic cat breed.

| Member | Real name | Quirk | Birthday |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mandalay** (leader) | Shino Sosaki | Telepath — sends thoughts directly into others' minds | May 1 |
| **Pixie-Bob** | Ryuko Tsuchikawa | Earth Flow — manipulates soil and rock | June 26 |
| **Ragdoll** | Tomoko Shiretoko | Search — monitors up to 100 people at once, tracking their location and condition | April 8 |
| **Tiger** | Yawara Chatora | Pliabody — extends, flattens and reshapes his own body | February 29 |

Mandalay is **Kota Izumi's aunt**. Kota's parents were the Pro Hero duo **Water Hose**, who died in the line of duty — which is the source of his hatred of heroes, and which makes Mandalay's presence at the training camp quietly devastating.

During the Forest Training Camp attack, **Ragdoll's Search Quirk is stolen by All For One** — the first on-screen confirmation that Quirks can be permanently taken from a living person. Ragdoll is forced to retire from hero work and becomes the team's office assistant. Pixie-Bob, Mandalay and Tiger continue.

## What the Pro Heroes Represent

The professional hero body is the series' institutional subject, and Horikoshi writes it with unusual scepticism.

**The ranking system rewards visibility.** Mt. Lady says on camera, in episode 1, that she is in it for money and fame. She is not punished for it; she is ranked. Endeavor rises to No. 1 with lower approval than the two heroes below him because he resolves more incidents. The chart measures output, not character.

**The system is maintained by people the public never sees.** Lady Nagant carried out assassinations for the Hero Public Safety Commission. Hawks was raised as an operative rather than a child. Both institutions exist to preserve peace, and both do it with methods that would destroy the peace if they were public.

**And the heroes themselves are mostly decent.** Fat Gum, Ryukyu, Kamui Woods, Selkie and Manual are all competent, unglamorous and kind. The series' critique is aimed at the structure, not at the individuals inside it — which is what makes Stain's argument so difficult to dismiss, and so difficult to accept.
