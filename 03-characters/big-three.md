# The Big Three

*U.A.'s three strongest third-year students — Mirio Togata, Tamaki Amajiki, and Nejire Hado. Introduced formally in Season 4 as the standard the first-years are measured against.*

The Big Three are the series' answer to a specific question: what do students who have *already* survived three years of U.A. look like? Each of the three represents a different relationship to talent and fear.

---

## Mirio Togata (通形 ミリオ) — *Lemillion*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Permeation |
| **Year** | 3rd year, U.A. Hero Course |
| **Voice (JP)** | Tarusuke Shingaki |
| **Voice (EN)** | Ricco Fajardo |
| **Debut** | Season 3 (cameo) / Season 4 |

**Quirk mechanics.** Permeation allows Mirio to phase any part of his body — or his whole body — through solid matter. The difficulty is severe: while phasing, he cannot see, hear, breathe, or feel, because light, sound, and air all pass through him. He must learn to predict his own re-emergence point. It took him years to become usable at all.

**Character.** Mirio is relentlessly cheerful, physically enormous, and completely without cynicism. He was personally groomed by **Sir Nighteye** — All Might's former sidekick — to be the next recipient of One For All, and Nighteye considered him a far better candidate than Midoriya. That he lost the role and then immediately became Midoriya's supporter, without a trace of resentment, is one of the clearest indicators of his character in the series.

**The Shie Hassaikai Arc.** Mirio's defining moment, and the most consequential event of Season 4.

During the raid on the Shie Hassaikai, Mirio fights **Overhaul** alone to give **Eri** time to escape. He is shot with a **Quirk-Destroying Bullet** and loses Permeation permanently. He keeps fighting anyway — unarmed, against an opponent who can disassemble his body on contact — for long enough for the rest of the raid team to arrive.

He is the strongest student in U.A., and he gives up everything he built over three years for a girl he met that morning.

**Afterwards.** Mirio does not retire. He continues his hero training without a Quirk, works out relentlessly, and eventually regains Permeation when Eri masters Rewind. His refusal to be defined by the loss is a thematic counterpoint to the series' treatment of All Might's retirement.

**Trivia.** His hero name "Lemillion" is a deliberate pun: *"le million"* — the man who will save a million people.

## Tamaki Amajiki (天喰 環) — *Suneater*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Manifest |
| **Year** | 3rd year, U.A. Hero Course |
| **Voice (JP)** | Yuto Uemura |
| **Voice (EN)** | Aaron Dismuke |
| **Debut** | Season 3 (cameo) / Season 4 |

**Quirk mechanics.** Manifest reproduces the physical characteristics of anything Tamaki has eaten. Eat octopus, grow tentacles; eat chicken, grow wings and talons; eat crystal, harden his skin. He can combine multiple foods at once. The Quirk's limitation is his memory and his stomach — he must have consumed the substance, and his recall degrades under stress.

**Character.** Tamaki is crippled by social anxiety. He cannot speak to strangers, faces walls when addressed, and describes himself as worthless. This is the series' sharpest irony: the boy with arguably the most versatile Quirk in U.A. is nearly unable to function in public.

Mirio is his only real anchor. The two have been friends since middle school, when Mirio defended him, and Tamaki's entire heroic identity is built on the effort to stop disappointing him.

**Development.** During the Shie Hassaikai raid Tamaki fights **Chronostasis** and several yakuza elites, holding a corridor alone against multiple opponents. Episode 71, "Suneater of the Big Three," is his showcase: he overcomes a panic attack mid-battle by imagining he is talking to a small audience rather than a crowd.

By the Paranormal Liberation War he is fighting High-End Nomu alongside Pro Heroes.

**Trivia.** His hero name "Suneater" was chosen by Mirio, who thought Tamaki would look like a hero who devours the sun. Tamaki hated it and has been stuck with it ever since.

## Nejire Hado (波動 ねじれ) — *Nejire Chan*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Wave Motion |
| **Year** | 3rd year, U.A. Hero Course |
| **Voice (JP)** | Kiyono Yasuno |
| **Voice (EN)** | Lindsay Seidel |
| **Debut** | Season 3 (cameo) / Season 4 |

**Quirk mechanics.** Nejire converts her own vitality into spiralling shockwaves that she can fire from her hands and feet, and use to fly. The waves travel in a helix, giving them greater piercing force than a straight blast. The limitation is output — she must generate continuously, and sustained use exhausts her quickly.

**Character.** Nejire is curious to the point of social obliviousness. She asks strangers invasive personal questions at close range, touches people's hair without permission, and narrates her own thought process out loud. She has no filter and no malice — the combination is what makes her likeable rather than obnoxious.

Her hero name, "Nejire Chan," is simply her own name with an affectionate suffix, chosen because she could not think of anything else and liked how it sounded.

**Development.** Nejire is the least explored of the Big Three in the early seasons but becomes increasingly prominent from Season 5 onward. She fights in the Paranormal Liberation War and, in the Final War arc, is one of the students who sustains the most serious injuries — which the series uses to show how far the conflict has escalated beyond anything a teenager should face.

## The Big Three as a Structural Device

Horikoshi introduces the Big Three at a specific narrative moment: immediately after All Might's retirement. Their function is to answer the question the audience is asking — *who replaces him?*

The answer the series gives is deliberately unsatisfying. The three strongest students in U.A. are:
- one who loses his Quirk entirely,
- one who cannot speak to people, and
- one who does not understand social boundaries.

None of them is a Symbol of Peace. That is the point. The Big Three exist to demonstrate that the next generation will not produce another All Might — and that a society waiting for one is a society that will not survive.
