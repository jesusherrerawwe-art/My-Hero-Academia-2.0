# The Big Three

*U.A.'s three strongest third-year students — Mirio Togata, Tamaki Amajiki, and Nejire Hado. Introduced in **Season 3, episodes 62–63**, they are the standard the first-years are measured against.*

The Big Three are the series' answer to a specific question: what do students who have *already* survived three years of U.A. look like? Each of the three represents a different relationship to talent and fear — and none of them looks like a Symbol of Peace.

They are formally presented to Class 1-A and Class 1-B in episode 62, *"A Season for Encounters,"* and in episode 63, *"Unrivaled,"* Mirio demonstrates why the title applies by defeating both first-year classes essentially single-handed.

---

## Mirio Togata (通形 ミリオ) — *Lemillion*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Permeation |
| **Year** | 3rd year, U.A. Hero Course |
| **Birthday** | July 15 — the same day as Izuku Midoriya |
| **Height** | 181 cm |
| **Voice (JP)** | Tarusuke Shingaki |
| **Voice (EN)** | Ricco Fajardo |
| **Debut** | Season 3, episode 62 |

**Quirk mechanics.** Permeation allows Mirio to phase any part of his body — or his whole body — through solid matter. The difficulty is severe and the series is explicit about it: while phasing, he cannot see, hear, breathe or feel, because light, sound and air all pass through him. His clothes fall through him too, so his costume is made from his own hair woven into fibres that respond to his Quirk. He must learn to predict his own re-emergence point entirely by memory and timing.

He describes it as a Quirk that is **worse than useless without years of work**, and that is the point: Mirio is not talented. He is a man with a badly designed power who decided to master it anyway. All Might calls him, without qualification, the student closest to becoming the next Symbol of Peace.

**Character.** Mirio is relentlessly cheerful, physically enormous, and completely without cynicism. He was personally groomed by **Sir Nighteye** — All Might's former sidekick — to be the next recipient of One For All, and Nighteye considered him a far better candidate than Midoriya. That he lost the role and then immediately became Midoriya's supporter, without a trace of resentment, is one of the clearest indicators of his character in the series.

**The Shie Hassaikai Arc.** Mirio's defining moment, and the most consequential event of Season 4.

During the raid on the Shie Hassaikai, Mirio fights **Overhaul** alone in a corridor to give **Eri** time to escape. Chisaki can disassemble a human body on contact; Mirio can phase through anything. It should not be a contest. Chisaki shoots him with a **Quirk-Destroying Bullet**.

**Mirio loses Permeation permanently.** He is Quirkless — exactly what Midoriya was in episode 1 — and he keeps fighting anyway, unarmed, against an opponent who can take him apart by touching him, for long enough for the rest of the raid team to arrive.

He is the strongest student in U.A., and he gives up everything he built over three years for a girl he met that morning. When Midoriya asks him afterwards whether he regrets it, Mirio says no, and means it.

**Afterwards.** Mirio does not retire. He continues his hero training without a Quirk, works out relentlessly, and even turns down Midoriya's offer to pass him One For All. Roughly six months later, during the **Paranormal Liberation War** (manga chapter 363, *"Light Fades to Rain"*), Eri — having trained Rewind on bugs and lizards — restores Permeation, and Mirio returns to the battlefield at Jaku. In the series' epilogue he is the **No. 1 Hero in Japan**.

**Trivia.** His hero name "Lemillion" is a deliberate pun on *le million* — the man who will save a million people. He chose it as a promise rather than a boast, and the season in which he loses everything is the season in which he keeps it.

## Tamaki Amajiki (天喰 環) — *Suneater*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Manifest |
| **Year** | 3rd year, U.A. Hero Course |
| **Birthday** | March 4 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Yuto Uemura |
| **Voice (EN)** | Aaron Dismuke |
| **Debut** | Season 3, episode 62 |

**Quirk mechanics.** Manifest reproduces the physical characteristics of anything Tamaki has eaten. Eat octopus, grow tentacles; eat chicken, grow wings and talons; eat clam and crab, grow a shell and pincers; eat crystal, harden his skin. He can combine multiple foods at once, producing composite forms no single organism has.

The Quirk's limitation is his memory and his stomach: he must have consumed the substance, and his recall degrades badly under stress — which, for Tamaki, means almost constantly.

**Character.** Tamaki is crippled by social anxiety. He cannot speak to strangers, faces walls when addressed, apologises for existing, and describes himself as worthless. This is the series' sharpest irony: the boy with arguably the most versatile Quirk in U.A. is nearly unable to function in public.

Mirio is his anchor. The two have been friends since middle school, when Mirio defended him, and Tamaki's entire heroic identity is built on the effort to stop disappointing him. He is also the more tactically perceptive of the two — his anxiety makes him hyper-observant, and he frequently notices threats before anyone else does.

**Development.** During the Shie Hassaikai raid Tamaki holds a corridor **alone against three of the Eight Bullets** — Toya Setsuno (Larceny), Yu Hojo (Crystallize) and Soramitsu Tabe (Food) — and beats all three.

Episode 71, *"Suneater of the Big Three,"* is his showcase. He overcomes a panic attack mid-battle using a technique he developed with Mirio: imagining that he is speaking to one person rather than to a crowd. The episode's structure is deliberately inverted — the strongest student in U.A. is being talked down from a panic attack while fighting for his life, and both things are true at once.

He takes his work-study with **Fat Gum** alongside Kirishima. By the Paranormal Liberation War he is fighting High-End Nomu alongside Pro Heroes.

**Trivia.** He is profoundly embarrassed by his hero name and has been unable to escape it.

## Nejire Hado (波動 ねじれ) — *Nejire Chan*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Wave Motion |
| **Year** | 3rd year, U.A. Hero Course |
| **Birthday** | October 6 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Kiyono Yasuno |
| **Voice (EN)** | Lindsay Seidel |
| **Debut** | Season 3, episode 62 |

**Quirk mechanics.** Nejire converts her own vitality into spiralling shockwaves that she fires from her hands and feet, and uses to fly. The waves travel in a helix rather than straight, which gives them greater piercing force and lets them wrap around obstacles. Her signature technique, **Nejire Flood**, releases the shockwave as an expanding spiral.

The limitation is output: the power comes from her own stamina, so sustained use exhausts her quickly, and she must generate continuously rather than store.

**Character.** Nejire is curious to the point of social obliviousness. She asks strangers invasive personal questions at close range, touches people's hair without permission, narrates her own thought process out loud, and floats around people while inspecting them. She has no filter and no malice — the combination is what makes her likeable rather than obnoxious.

Her hero name is simply her own name with an affectionate suffix, chosen because she could not think of anything else and liked how it sounded.

**Development.** Nejire is the least explored of the Big Three in the early seasons, but she is by far the most consistently effective in combat. She takes her work-study with **Ryukyu** alongside Uraraka and Asui, and becomes Ryukyu's most trusted field operative.

She fights throughout the Paranormal Liberation War and, in the final arcs, is one of the students who sustains the most serious injuries — which the series uses to show how far the conflict has escalated beyond anything a teenager should face. In the epilogue she reaches **No. 7** on the Hero Billboard Chart, the highest-ranked of the three Big Three still operating under her own name rather than a legacy.

## The Big Three as a Structural Device

Horikoshi introduces the Big Three at a very specific narrative moment: immediately after All Might's retirement, and immediately before the work-study system puts Class 1-A in contact with real agencies. Their function is to answer the question the audience is asking — *who replaces him?*

The answer the series gives is deliberately unsatisfying. The three strongest students in U.A. are:

- one who loses his Quirk entirely and keeps going,
- one who cannot speak to people, and
- one who does not understand social boundaries.

None of them is a Symbol of Peace. That is the point. The Big Three exist to demonstrate that the next generation will not produce another All Might — and that a society waiting for one is a society that will not survive.

There is also a quieter argument in how they are written. Mirio's Permeation is useless without years of work; Tamaki's Manifest is useless without the nerve to use it; Nejire's Wave Motion drains her the moment she relies on it. **None of the three has a Quirk that solves anything on its own.** Compared with Todoroki, who was engineered, and Midoriya, who was handed the largest inheritance in the world, the Big Three are the series' proof that competence is built rather than given.
