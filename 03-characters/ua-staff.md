# U.A. High School Staff

*The faculty of Japan's most prestigious hero academy.*

U.A. employs Pro Heroes as teaching staff across four courses — **Hero**, **Support**, **General Studies** and **Management**. The faculty below are the ones who appear regularly in Seasons 1–5.

> **Spoiler note:** entries flagged ⚠️ describe events beyond Season 5.

---

## Toshinori Yagi (八木 俊典) — *All Might*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | One For All (former holder) |
| **Birthday** | June 10 |
| **Height** | 220 cm in hero form |
| **Role** | Heroics teacher, Class 1-A (Seasons 1–3); faculty adviser thereafter |
| **Voice (JP)** | Kenta Miyake |
| **Voice (EN)** | Christopher Sabat |
| **Debut** | Chapter 1 / Episode 1 |

The **Symbol of Peace** and, for most of the series, the No. 1 Hero in Japan. All Might is the eighth wielder of **One For All**, inheriting it from his mentor **Nana Shimura**. He held the top rank for years and single-handedly suppressed violent crime across the country — not through policing, but through the psychological effect of his presence.

**The secret.** All Might was grievously wounded in his first battle with **All For One**, roughly five years before the series begins: his stomach was removed in surgery and much of his respiratory system, including a lung, destroyed. Since then he has been able to maintain his muscular hero form for only a few hours per day — later, only minutes, and by Season 3 only seconds at a time. In his true form he is **Toshinori Yagi**: a skeletal, gaunt man who coughs blood and looks nothing like the hero on every billboard in Japan.

This is the structural engine of the entire series. All Might is performing invulnerability. Every smile is an act of maintenance — he smiles so that people will not be afraid.

**Character.** He is theatrical, boisterous, and completely sincere. He also makes a series of serious mistakes: he chooses Midoriya impulsively, on the basis of a single act; he conceals his condition from nearly everyone around him; and his single-minded pursuit of All For One leaves his mentor's family unaddressed, which is how Tenko Shimura ends up in All For One's hands.

**Kamino Ward (Season 3, episodes 47–49).** His final battle. Already operating past his time limit, he fights All For One on live television, wins with a single **United States of Smash**, and collapses into his true form in front of the entire country. He points at the camera and says, *"Next… it's your turn."* Then he retires.

**After retirement.** He becomes a U.A. faculty member and Midoriya's full-time mentor, working to help him master One For All's inherited Quirks. He is no longer a symbol; he is a man trying to prepare someone else. He also, finally, tells Katsuki Bakugo the truth about One For All — and apologises to him, which Bakugo is not equipped to receive.

**Trivia.** His name, 八木 俊典, is deliberately ordinary. "All Might" was chosen to sound like an American comic book, which is precisely the effect Nana Shimura's generation wanted.

## Shota Aizawa (相澤 消太) — *Eraser Head*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Erasure |
| **Birthday** | November 8 |
| **Height** | 183 cm |
| **Age** | 30 |
| **Role** | Class 1-A homeroom teacher |
| **Voice (JP)** | Junichi Suwabe |
| **Voice (EN)** | Alex Organ (Season 1); Christopher Wehkamp (Season 2 onward) |
| **Debut** | Chapter 5 / Episode 4 |

Aizawa's Quirk nullifies any other Quirk he can see. It does not work on mutation-type Quirks — he cannot erase a frog physiology or a tail — and maintaining it causes severe dry eye, so he must blink periodically. He fights using a **capture weapon**, a carbon-fibre binding cloth, combined with close-quarters technique, making him effective precisely because he removes his opponent's advantage rather than overpowering them.

**Character.** Aizawa is exhausted, blunt, and openly contemptuous of hero theatrics. He sleeps in a yellow sleeping bag between classes and drinks pouches of jelly instead of eating. He is also, by a considerable margin, the most protective teacher at U.A.

His defining philosophy is **rationality as care**. In episode 4 he administers a Quirk Apprehension Test and threatens to expel whoever places last, revealing afterward that the threat was "a logical deception" — but it is later revealed that he once expelled an entire first-year homeroom class, then quietly re-enrolled them once they took his warnings seriously. He tells Midoriya that a hero who breaks his own body to save one person is useless if he dies doing it.

**Backstory.** Aizawa lost his close friend **Oboro Shirakumo** while they were students at U.A.; Shirakumo died in a villain attack, and Aizawa and Hizashi Yamada never recovered from it. Years later, Shirakumo's body was used by Doctor Garaki to create **Kurogiri**, the League of Villains' warp-gate Nomu. Aizawa spends his entire career not knowing this.

⚠️ In Season 5, episode 107, he and Yamada interrogate Kurogiri in Tartarus and discover the truth. Aizawa breaks down. It is one of the most emotionally devastating sequences in the series, and it exists to make the coming war personal for him specifically.

**Forest Training Camp (Season 3).** He moves through the forest efficiently neutralising dozens of the Vanguard Action Squad's low-tier villains and clashes twice with Dabi (erasing his fire; the first is a Twice clone). He takes cuts and bruises but is not maimed here, and he is on his feet directing the clean-up afterwards. Moonfish is fought by Bakugo, Todoroki and the berserk Dark Shadow, not by Aizawa.

> ⚠️ **A common misconception.** Aizawa does *not* lose his eye or his leg in Season 3. That happens considerably later, during the **Paranormal Liberation War** (Season 6): a Quirk-Destroying Bullet is about to hit him and he amputates his own **right** leg to stop the drug spreading; Shigaraki then damages his right eye. He subsequently uses a prosthetic right leg and an eyepatch, and retains only **limited** use of Erasure through his remaining eye (the heroes later work around this by having Monoma copy it).

## Nezu (根津) — *Principal*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | High Spec |
| **Birthday** | January 1 |
| **Height** | 85 cm |
| **Voice (JP)** | Yasuhiro Takato |
| **Voice (EN)** | Jerry Jewell |

An animal — apparently a dog, a mouse or a bear; he declines to specify — who was given human-level intelligence through experimentation before Quirks were widespread. **High Spec** grants him intellect far beyond any human's.

Nezu is polite, tea-drinking, and quietly ruthless. He designed U.A.'s current structure, including its barrier system and the dorm programme introduced after Kamino. He is the character who most clearly understands that U.A. is a target, and who acts on that understanding before anyone else does.

He is also the only faculty member who defeats students in the Final Exams through pure intellect rather than combat: paired against Ashido and Kaminari, he traps them in a maze they cannot solve, and both fail. The lesson is that raw power is not the same as capability.

Nezu hints repeatedly at a painful past connected to the experiments that created him, and the series never fully resolves it.

## Hizashi Yamada (山田 陽射) — *Present Mic*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Voice |
| **Birthday** | July 7 |
| **Height** | 185 cm |
| **Age** | 30 |
| **Role** | English teacher; event announcer |
| **Voice (JP)** | Hiroyuki Yoshino |
| **Voice (EN)** | Sonny Strait |

Amplifies his voice into a directional sonic attack, with enough power to shatter concrete at range. He is U.A.'s English teacher and the announcer for every major event, speaking in an exaggerated radio-DJ register — "**YEEEEAH!**" — and shouting the names of students' techniques as though calling a wrestling match.

Beneath the performance, Yamada is Aizawa's oldest friend and the series' most emotionally articulate adult. He and Aizawa were classmates with **Oboro Shirakumo**, and Yamada carries that loss openly in a way Aizawa cannot.

⚠️ In Season 5 it is Yamada, not Aizawa, who breaks through to Kurogiri — by calling him *Oboro*. He is also the character who most consistently pushes the faculty toward treating the students as children rather than assets, and who objects loudest when the Hero Public Safety Commission tries to use them.

## Nemuri Kayama (香山 眠) — *Midnight*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Somnambulist |
| **Birthday** | March 9 |
| **Height** | 175 cm |
| **Age** | 31 |
| **Role** | Modern Hero Art History teacher |
| **Voice (JP)** | Akeno Watanabe |
| **Voice (EN)** | Elizabeth Maxwell |

Emits a sleep-inducing aroma from her skin. Men are affected far more strongly than women, which makes her Quirk situationally unreliable and which the series plays for comedy in the Final Exams.

Midnight is confident, flirtatious, and theatrical, and she is unapologetic about the fact that her hero persona is built on her appearance. She is also a genuinely capable combatant, and she referees the U.A. Sports Festival and announces the Joint Training results.

She is one of the faculty's most decisive members during the U.S.J. incident.

⚠️ **Spoiler:** Midnight dies during the Paranormal Liberation War, in Season 6. Her death is one of the faculty casualties the series uses to make clear that the war is not survivable by everyone.

## Anan Kurose (黒瀬 穴南) — *Thirteen*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Black Hole |
| **Birthday** | February 3 |
| **Height** | 180 cm |
| **Age** | 28 |
| **Role** | Rescue specialist; designed the U.S.J. |
| **Voice (JP)** | Inuko Inuyama |
| **Voice (EN)** | Morgan Berry |

The rescue specialist who designed and operates the **Unforeseen Simulation Joint**. Thirteen's Quirk creates miniature black holes from their fingertips capable of disintegrating anything — a power they almost never use offensively, having chosen a rescue career instead.

Thirteen wears a spacesuit-style costume because their Quirk would destroy their own body if used carelessly. During the U.S.J. attack they attempt to trap Kurogiri with Black Hole; Kurogiri opens a warp gate *inside* the attack, and Thirteen's own Quirk is redirected into their body. They survive only through Recovery Girl's treatment, and spend the rest of Season 1 hospitalised.

The exchange is the series' cleanest statement of its central tactical idea: **a Quirk turned against its user is the most reliable way to beat them.** Kurogiri wins by understanding Thirteen better than Thirteen understands himself.

Thirteen's gender is never specified in the series, and the character is written with deliberately neutral pronouns.

## Chiyo Shuzenji (修復 千代) — *Recovery Girl*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Heal |
| **Birthday** | April 4 |
| **Height** | 115 cm |
| **Age** | 60+ |
| **Role** | School nurse |
| **Voice (JP)** | Etsuko Kozakura |
| **Voice (EN)** | Juli Erickson (Season 1); Luci Christian (Season 2 onward) |

U.A.'s school nurse. Her Quirk **does not create health** — by kissing the injury she stimulates the target's natural healing to work at accelerated speed, but the effect runs on the **patient's own stamina**, not hers. If the patient has no energy left, she cannot heal them, and she cannot regrow limbs or restore what is already gone. Repeated treatment exhausts the patient as much as the injury does.

She is elderly, short-tempered, and the only person at U.A. who regularly scolds All Might to his face. She kisses patients' foreheads to activate Heal, which the students find deeply embarrassing.

Her repeated warnings to Midoriya — that she cannot keep fixing him, that he will eventually run out of body to heal, that there is a limit past which she will refuse to treat him — are the series' most consistent moral voice on self-sacrifice. She is right every time, and Midoriya ignores her every time.

## Sekijiro Kan (管 赤慈郎) — *Vlad King*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Blood Control |
| **Birthday** | November 9 |
| **Role** | Class 1-B homeroom teacher |
| **Voice (JP)** | Shuhei Matsuda |
| **Voice (EN)** | Ben Bryant |

Manipulates his own blood outside his body, hardening it into constructs, blades and barriers. Class 1-B's homeroom teacher.

Vlad King is Aizawa's professional counterpart and occasional rival. He is considerably more conventional in his teaching style — supportive, straightforward, and openly proud of his class — and he defends Class 1-B against the implication that they matter less, while privately agreeing with Monoma that they deserve more recognition than they get.

He co-referees the Joint Training Battle with Aizawa, and it is Vlad King who announces that Hitoshi Shinso has been accepted into the Hero Course.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Cement |
| **Birthday** | March 22 |
| **Age** | 28 |
| **Role** | Modern Literature teacher |
| **Voice (JP)** | Kenta Okuma |
| **Voice (EN)** | Chris Rager |

Controls any cement-based material, raising walls, pillars and floors at will. U.A.'s Modern Literature teacher and a highly effective area-control combatant.

## The Faculty as a Whole

The U.A. staff is unusually combat-capable for a teaching body, and the series uses that fact twice. During the **U.S.J. attack** Aizawa and Thirteen fight a coordinated villain invasion essentially alone, and Aizawa is nearly killed. During the **Forest Training Camp** the faculty arrives too late — because the League knew exactly where the students would be.

Both incidents produce the same conclusion, which Nezu acts on and the Hero Public Safety Commission resists: U.A. cannot protect its students by being a school. The dorms, the barrier upgrades and the eventual decision to treat Class 1-A as combatants rather than children all follow from it.
