# Class 1-A

*The twenty first-year students of U.A. High School's Hero Course, homeroom class of Shota Aizawa (Eraser Head). The protagonists of the series.*

## Main Characters

### Izuku Midoriya (緑谷 出久) — *Deku*

**Quirk:** One For All · **Debut:** Chapter 1 / Episode 1

The protagonist. Born Quirkless into a world where powers are the norm, Midoriya spent his childhood being told his dream was impossible — most cruelly by Katsuki Bakugo, who once advised him to jump off a roof and hope for a Quirk in his next life. Rather than become bitter, he became an obsessive student of heroism, filling notebook after notebook — by the start of the series he is on **"Hero Analysis for the Future, No. 13"** — with detailed analysis of every Pro Hero's abilities and tactics.

All Might chooses him not for talent but for one specific act: charging a sludge villain to save Bakugo while every licensed professional stood by. Midoriya inherits **One For All**, a power stockpiled across nine generations of wielders.

**Abilities.** One For All grants enormous superhuman strength, speed and durability. Midoriya's body cannot initially withstand it — he breaks bones on nearly every early use — so he develops, in order:

| Technique | Learned | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Full Cowl** | Season 2, with Gran Torino | Circulates One For All through the whole body at a low percentage constantly, instead of firing it from one limb |
| **Shoot Style** | Season 3 | Focuses power in the legs to spare his repeatedly shattered arms |
| **Iron Soles** | Season 3 (Costume γ, built by Hatsume) | Reinforced shoes for full-power kicks, paired with Shoot Style |
| **Air Force Gloves** | Season 4 (built by Hatsume) | Gloves that fire compressed-air projectiles at range: the **Delaware Smash Air Force** |
| **Blackwhip** | Season 5 (Joint Training, Round 5) | Dark tendrils from the 5th user, Daigoro Banjo — the *first* additional Quirk to manifest |

By the end of the story, One For All has accumulated enough power to grant Midoriya access to **six additional Quirks** belonging to previous holders (only Blackwhip manifests during Season 5; the others surface in Season 6 and the final war), each stronger in his hands than it was in theirs:

| Quirk | Holder | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Gearshift** | 2nd user | Alters the speed and trajectory of whatever it touches |
| **Fa Jin** | 3rd user | Stores kinetic energy from repeated movement and releases it explosively |
| **Danger Sense** | 4th — Hikage Shinomori | Detects hostile intent |
| **Blackwhip** | 5th — Daigoro Banjo | Dark energy tendrils controlled by willpower |
| **Smokescreen** | 6th — En | Generates obscuring smoke |
| **Float** | 7th — Nana Shimura | Levitation |

**Character.** Midoriya mutters constantly when analysing situations, cries easily, and apologises excessively. His defining trait is that he **moves before he thinks** when someone is in danger — the quality All Might required. The series' central critique of him is that this instinct is pathological: he will destroy himself to save one person, and in later arcs his attempt to protect everyone by working alone is treated as a serious mistake rather than a virtue.

**Trivia.** His hero name "Deku" was originally Bakugo's insult — a homophone for 木偶 (*deku*), "wooden doll," and by extension "useless." He adopts it after Uraraka tells him it can also be read as できる (*dekiru*), "I can do it."

### Katsuki Bakugo (爆豪 勝己) — *Dynamight*

**Quirk:** Explosion · **Debut:** Chapter 1 / Episode 1

Midoriya's childhood friend and tormentor. Bakugo's Quirk lets him secrete nitroglycerin-like sweat from his palms and detonate it, producing concussive blasts powerful enough to propel him through the air and level buildings. Because the sweat accumulates, he is stronger in summer and after exertion.

For most of the series Bakugo is the closest thing the protagonist has to an antagonist. He is arrogant, foul-tempered, and openly cruel, driven by a single goal: to surpass All Might and become the undisputed No. 1 Hero. His hatred of Midoriya stems from a specific psychological wound — a powerless boy kept trying to help him, which Bakugo experienced as being looked down on.

**Signature techniques.**

| Technique | Description |
|---|---|
| **Howitzer Impact** | Uses explosions to create a tornado, then detonates the oxygen-depleted centre |
| **AP Shot** | Concentrates a blast into a narrow piercing beam |
| **Cluster** | Chains small explosions to accelerate mid-air |
| **Stun Grenade** | A blinding close-range detonation |

**Character arc.** Bakugo's development is the slowest and hardest-won in the series. He is kidnapped by the League of Villains and refuses to turn, which nearly costs him his life and — because All Might burns out his remaining power rescuing him, on live television — costs him the country's goodwill. He never learns the truth in Season 2: that happens in **Season 3, episodes 60–61, *"Deku vs. Kacchan, Part 2"* at Ground Beta**, where All Might explains everything and Bakugo breaks down.

He and Todoroki are the two students who **fail the Provisional Hero Licence Exam** on conduct grounds, and both are made to retrain under Gang Orca. In the Joint Training arc (Season 5) he fights with a team for the first time without treating them as obstacles, and afterwards tells them, flatly, that they did well.

By the series' end he has become Midoriya's most reliable ally and the person who most consistently refuses to let him sacrifice himself. He apologises to Midoriya for years of abuse — a moment the series treats as genuine but insufficient, and not as a resolution.

**Trivia.** At the Season 2 hero-naming exercise Bakugo proposes **"King Explosion Murder"** (then, when rejected, "Lord Explosion Murder"); **Midnight rejects both** as villain names and he leaves without registering anything. He keeps his eventual choice secret for years: **Great Explosion Murder God: Dynamight**, first revealed to Best Jeanist during the Paranormal Liberation War (manga chapter 293).

### Shoto Todoroki (轟 焦凍) — *Shoto*

**Quirk:** Half-Cold Half-Hot · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

The son of Endeavor. Todoroki's Quirk gives him ice on his right side and fire on his left — a combination deliberately engineered by his father, who married a woman with an ice Quirk specifically to produce a child who could surpass All Might.

The cost was his family. Endeavor's abuse drove Todoroki's mother, Rei, to a breakdown; she poured boiling water on the left side of his face, leaving the burn scar he still carries. For most of the series Todoroki refuses to use his fire half entirely, viewing it as his father's property rather than his own.

**Character arc.** The Sports Festival is his turning point. Midoriya, already broken from earlier matches, shouts *"It's your power, isn't it?!"* — and Todoroki uses his flames for the first time. It costs him the championship: he stops mid-attack, remembering his mother, and Bakugo wins a final Todoroki refused to finish.

What follows is not a reconciliation but a long, uncomfortable process. He visits his mother in hospital. He speaks to his sister Fuyumi. His brother Natsuo refuses to forgive their father and says so openly. He takes a work-study at Endeavor's agency in Season 5 specifically to watch whether his father changes. He learns **Flashfire Fist** techniques. And in Season 6 he faces **Dabi**, revealed to be his eldest brother Toya, presumed dead in a fire more than a decade earlier.

Todoroki is also one of the two students who fail the Provisional Licence Exam — not for weakness but because Inasa Yoarashi correctly identifies in him the same cold hostility he despises in Endeavor.

**Trivia.** His given name 焦凍 combines 焦 ("scorch") and 凍 ("freeze") — his Quirk written into his own name. He uses his given name as his hero name, refusing anything connected to his father.

## Secondary Characters

### Ochaco Uraraka (麗日 お茶子) — *Uravity*

**Quirk:** Zero Gravity · **Debut:** Season 1 — U.A. Entrance Exam

Nullifies gravity in anything she touches with the pads of her fingertips, releasing the effect by pressing her fingers together. Overuse causes severe nausea. She can also release the effect on other people's targets, which makes her an unusually strong support fighter.

Uraraka is warm, direct, and unusually pragmatic — she became a hero explicitly to earn money for her parents' struggling construction business, which she states without embarrassment. She is Midoriya's first friend at U.A. and the person who turns his insult into his hero name.

Her combat development runs through her internship with the Pro Hero **Gunhead**, whose close-quarters style turns her from a support fighter into a genuinely dangerous grappler — she calls it **Gunhead Martial Arts** and uses it on Monoma in Season 5.

Her later arc is the series' most direct confrontation with the question of whether villains deserve help. She is the character who addresses **Himiko Toga** not as an enemy but as a girl who was told her feelings were monstrous, and argues that she should have been helped rather than hunted.

---

### Tenya Iida (飯田 天哉) — *Ingenium*

**Quirk:** Engine · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 4

Engine-like exhaust ports in his calves grant him extraordinary speed. He is Class 1-A's representative — earnest, rule-bound, and given to emphatic chopping hand gestures.

Iida comes from a hero family; his older brother **Tensei** was the Pro Hero Ingenium until the Hero Killer Stain crippled him permanently. Iida's arc in Season 2 is a study in grief: he takes a work-study in Hosu City purely to hunt Stain alone, and is cut down within seconds of finding him. Midoriya and Todoroki save his life, and he learns that his brother's understanding of heroism — protecting others rather than pursuing personal justice — was the correct one.

His technique develops across the series:

| Technique | Description |
|---|---|
| **Recipro Burst** | A maximum-output sprint lasting only **ten seconds**, first used in the Sports Festival cavalry battle to steal the 10-million-point headband; afterwards the engines stall |
| **Recipro Turbo** | A refined family technique, developed for the Joint Training Match 3 (ep. 95): his new mufflers let Recipro run for **ten minutes** at speeds said to surpass Gran Torino's |

He later inherits the Ingenium name, though the series is careful to show that he carries it with ambivalence rather than pride. He is one of the two students tied for third at the Sports Festival, alongside Tokoyami.

---

### Eijiro Kirishima (切島 鋭児郎) — *Red Riot*

**Quirk:** Hardening · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Hardens his skin into a rock-like substance, granting high defence at the cost of mobility. With training he develops **Red Riot Unbreakable**, a far more durable form that leaves his skin jagged and red.

Kirishima is loud, friendly, and obsessed with the idea of **manliness** — which he defines specifically as the willingness to stand in front of others. His backstory, revealed in Season 4, is that he was once timid and self-loathing, and reinvented himself around the Pro Hero **Crimson Riot**, whose philosophy was to face danger head-on with a grin. Kirishima's hardening is, in a sense, a personality he built deliberately.

He is Bakugo's closest friend and the person who most consistently reaches him emotionally. During the Bakugo rescue he is one of the five students who break the rules — and when he hesitates at the last moment, Bakugo shouts at him for it, which is the closest thing to an acknowledgment Bakugo ever gives him.

In Season 4 he takes a work-study with **Fat Gum** in Osaka and fights **Kendo Rappa**, holding a machine-gun-speed puncher off long enough for Fat Gum to win. It is the moment Kirishima stops performing bravery and starts having it.

He is also one of the five students who **fail the Season 2 final exams** (paired with Sato against Cementoss), which does not exclude him from the summer training camp — Aizawa simply insists the failures need it most.

### Momo Yaoyorozu (八百万 百) — *Creati*

**Quirk:** Creation · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Creates any non-living object she can visualise the molecular structure of, drawing on her body's lipid reserves. She must understand the object's composition completely, which makes her the class's most academically demanding Quirk. The richest student in Class 1-A and its vice-representative.

Yaoyorozu is exceptionally intelligent — arguably the smartest tactician in the class — but suffers acute self-doubt. Her crisis is dramatised in the Final Exams: paired with Todoroki against Aizawa, every plan she proposes, he executes better and faster, until she stops proposing any. They pass only because she creates the capture bands that restrain Aizawa — a plan she insists was his. Aizawa tells her afterward that she has never lacked ability, only conviction, and that she stopped trusting herself the moment a stronger partner appeared beside her.

That lesson takes the rest of the series to land. By the Paranormal Liberation War she is commanding groups of Pro Heroes, and in the Joint Training arc her team **loses 4–0** to Class 1-B — a deliberate reminder that intelligence without self-trust still loses.

### Tsuyu Asui (蛙吹 梅雨) — *Froppy*

**Quirk:** Frog · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Full frog physiology: adhesive pads, a prehensile tongue roughly twenty metres long, enhanced jumping, camouflage, the ability to flatten herself, and a stomach she can evert. She is highly sensitive to cold, which significantly weakens her.

Asui is blunt to the point of bluntness-as-kindness. She says exactly what she thinks, calls people by their given names with a *-chan* suffix, and is the emotional anchor of Class 1-A's female students.

She survives the U.S.J. attack in the shipwreck zone through tactics rather than strength, protecting Mineta and herself against water villains who outnumber them. Her most important character moment comes when she tells Midoriya plainly that she has noticed he is lying about his Quirk — and that she will not press him, because being his friend matters more than knowing.

She takes a work-study with **Ryukyu** alongside Uraraka, and is one of the most consistently reliable students in the class.

---

### Fumikage Tokoyami (常闇 踏陰) — *Tsukuyomi*

**Quirk:** Dark Shadow · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Hosts a sentient shadow entity named **Dark Shadow**, which becomes vastly more powerful — and far less controllable — in darkness. In bright light it is small and manageable; in total darkness it can grow large enough to level buildings, and it has its own will.

Tokoyami is theatrical, speaks in an archaic register, and is deeply devoted to his classmates. His Quirk is one of the most dangerous in Class 1-A precisely because of that autonomy: during the Forest Training Camp, Dark Shadow goes berserk in the night (after Moonfish cripples Shoji's tentacle) and is only brought back under control when Shoji shields him and Bakugo and Todoroki generate enough light to shrink the shadow.

He learns to master it through the series, developing techniques including **Black Abyss** (wearing Dark Shadow as armour), **Black Fallen Angel** (flight, carrying him through the air), **Ragnarök** (full darkness release) and **Light of Baldur** (a Black-Abyss-powered punch), the latter boosted by Inasa's wind in the final war. He ties for third place at the U.A. Sports Festival with Iida, and in Joint Training Round 2 he is on the Class 1-A team that loses 4–0 to Class 1-B — where **Kuroiro's** darkness manipulation proves a direct counter to his own.

### Kyoka Jiro (耳郎 響香) — *Earphone Jack*

**Quirk:** Earphone Jack · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Her earlobes are long, pluggable jacks that she can insert into surfaces to detect vibrations and heartbeats, or use to channel her own amplified heartbeat as a concussive sonic attack. She can also plug into other people to hear their pulse.

Jiro is a musician — bass guitar and vocals — who was initially ashamed of it, believing music was not a serious pursuit for a hero. Her arc concludes in the U.A. School Festival (Season 4), where she leads Class 1-A's live performance and accepts that what she loves is not a distraction from heroism but part of it.

The festival performance uses the character song **"Hero too,"** credited to *KYOKA JIRO starring Chrissy Costanza*.

She is one of the students who passes the Final Exams (paired with Koda against Present Mic) and fights effectively in the Joint Training arc as part of Bakugo's team.

---

### Denki Kaminari (上鳴 電気) — *Chargebolt*

**Quirk:** Electrification · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Generates and discharges electricity. His critical weakness: exceeding his wattage limit fries his own brain, leaving him in a vacant, thumbs-up-making state he calls "**wheeey**."

Kaminari is the class clown and self-professed ladies' man, and one of its weakest fighters early on — he loses to Ibara Shiozaki in the Sports Festival tournament because her vines simply ground his electricity into the earth. He develops Sharpshooting Gear that lets him **direct** his discharge precisely rather than omnidirectionally, which transforms him from a liability into a ranged specialist.

He **fails the Final Exams** alongside Ashido, beaten not by strength but by Principal Nezu's intellect. In Joint Training Round 1 he deliberately lets Shiozaki capture him as a decoy so Shinso can brainwash her, and is freed when his side wins — the two 1-A students who remain captured in that round are Koda and Kirishima. His raw output was also crucial to Team Todoroki's cavalry-battle victory in Season 2.

---

### Mina Ashido (芦戸 三奈) — *Pinky*

**Quirk:** Acid · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Secretes corrosive liquid from her skin with variable potency and viscosity; her body is immune to it. She can control its solvency precisely enough to melt through metal or to make it merely slippery. She has pink skin, black-and-yellow eyes, and small horns — a mutation-type presentation.

Ashido is energetic, athletic, and the class's best dancer. She **fails the Final Exams** with Kaminari and has among the lowest academic grades in Class 1-A, yet she is one of its most emotionally perceptive students — she is frequently the first to notice when a classmate is in trouble, and she dances in the School Festival performance.

In the Joint Training arc she is part of Midoriya's Round 5 team and, with Mineta, takes Nirengeki Shoda out of the 4–0 win.

---

### Hanta Sero (瀬呂 範太) — *Cellophane*

**Quirk:** Tape · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Dispenses strong, elastic cellophane-like tape from dispensers in his elbows. Excellent for mobility, restraint, and rescue — he can swing between buildings, bind multiple opponents at once, and catch falling civilians.

Sero is easygoing and functions as the class's informal mediator; he is usually the one who defuses tension between Bakugo and everyone else. Tactically he is one of the most useful support fighters in Class 1-A, though he rarely takes centre stage.

He is one of the five students who **fail the Final Exams** — paired with Mineta against Midnight, he is caught by her sleep gas after pushing Mineta out of the way, while Mineta turns the tables on Midnight with his balls and escapes the gate alone.

---

### Mashirao Ojiro (尾白 猿夫) — *Tailman*

**Quirk:** Tail · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

A strong, prehensile tail used for combat, balance and mobility; combined with genuine martial arts training, which makes him one of Class 1-A's most technically skilled hand-to-hand fighters.

Ojiro has one of the least flashy Quirks in the class, which the series explicitly treats as a source of insecurity for him. At the Sports Festival he advances to the final tournament as part of Team Shinso — then **voluntarily withdraws**, along with Nirengeki Shoda, because he was brainwashed and cannot remember earning his place. It is one of the most honourable decisions in the series, and it is also self-defeating: he gives up his only chance at recognition.

He fights in Round 3 of the Joint Training alongside Iida, Todoroki and Shoji, in the match that ends in a draw.

---

### Yuga Aoyama (青山 優雅) — *Can't Stop Twinkling*

**Quirk:** Navel Laser · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 4

Fires a sparkling laser from his navel. Overuse causes severe stomach pain, and his body is not built to withstand the Quirk at all — he frequently collapses after using it.

Aoyama is flamboyant, speaks in a mixture of Japanese and French, and obsessively centres himself in conversation. Beneath the performance is a deeply lonely boy who was **born Quirkless** — like Midoriya — and whose parents made a bargain with **All For One** to give him a power. The Navel Laser damages his body every time he uses it, and it was never meant for him.

> **Major spoiler.** Aoyama is the **U.A. traitor**. It is he who leaked the location of the Forest Training Camp to the League of Villains, on All For One's instructions, because his parents were being coerced. The revelation is one of the series' most significant late twists, and it reframes the Season 3 attack entirely — it was never a matter of the League finding U.A., but of U.A. being sold from inside.

He fights in Round 2 of the Joint Training, on the Class 1-A team that loses 4–0.

---

### Toru Hagakure (葉隠 透) — *Invisible Girl*

**Quirk:** Invisibility · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Permanently invisible. She can refract light through her body to produce blinding flashes — **Warp Refraction** — and later learns to bend light offensively and to conceal others.

Because her appearance is unknown for most of the series, Hagakure functions largely as comic relief and as a tactical wildcard: she is undetectable, which makes her ideal for stealth, reconnaissance and ambush. She is cheerful, direct, and unbothered by the attention her condition draws.

She passes the Final Exams paired with Shoji against Snipe, and is on Yaoyorozu's team in Joint Training Round 2.

---

### Mezo Shoji (障子 目蔵) — *Tentacole*

**Quirk:** Dupli-Arms · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 4

Can reproduce any of his organs at the ends of multiple replicating arms — eyes, ears, mouths, feet — giving him exceptional sensory range and combat versatility. He can generate new arms continuously.

Shoji wears a mask over the lower half of his face and is quiet and observant. He was bullied as a child for his appearance, and was specifically feared after an incident in which his duplicated arms frightened a young girl. His arc centres on learning that his body is not something to hide.

He is intensely protective of Tokoyami — during the Forest Training Camp he is the one who keeps Dark Shadow contained — and serves as the class's best reconnaissance asset. He fights in Round 3 of the Joint Training.

---

### Rikido Sato (砂藤 力道) — *Sugarman*

**Quirk:** Sugar Rush · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Gains a fivefold strength boost for a limited period after consuming sugar, at the cost of progressively impaired cognition the longer the boost lasts. He must keep eating to maintain it.

Sato is a large, gentle student and an excellent baker — he frequently makes cakes and sweets for the class, and his dorm room is effectively a patisserie. He is straightforward and dependable rather than dramatic, and the series uses him as an example of an ordinary, competent hero-in-training.

He **fails the Final Exams**, paired with Kirishima against Cementoss, who simply rebuilds every wall they break faster than they can break it.

---

### Koji Koda (口田 甲司) — *Anima*

**Quirk:** Anivoice · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Commands animals through speech. Later develops the ability to affect insects and to accelerate biological processes — including releasing pheromones that drive insects into a frenzy.

Koda is painfully shy and barely speaks, which creates an obvious tension with a Quirk that requires talking. He is the tallest student in Class 1-A at 186 cm and is frequently mistaken for being older.

His development across the series is about overcoming that fear, and specifically about his **aversion to insects**, which he must confront in order to use the strongest form of his Quirk. In Round 1 of the Joint Training he is trapped in a **Solid Air** prison by Kosei Tsuburaba, which prevents him from using Anivoice at all — a clean demonstration of how badly his Quirk depends on being able to speak.

---

### Minoru Mineta (峰田 実) — *Grape Juice*

**Quirk:** Pop Off · **Debut:** Chapter 5 / Episode 5

Plucks adhesive, bounce-capable spheres from his scalp and throws them; they stick to almost anything, can support considerable weight, and regrow. He can also use them as stepping stones and to create traps.

Mineta is short — at 108 cm he is by far the smallest student in U.A. — cowardly, and relentlessly lecherous. He is the series' most frequently criticised character, and deliberately so. His comedic function is undercut by moments of genuine tactical cleverness: he survives the U.S.J. shipwreck zone with Asui through ingenuity rather than strength, and he **passes the Final Exams** against Midnight while his partner Sero fails, because he is able to out-think a Quirk that should have beaten him easily.

He is also one of the few students who never improves significantly, which Horikoshi uses to ask an uncomfortable question: what happens when a person with no heroic qualities ends up in a heroic environment anyway?

In Joint Training Round 5 he and Mina handle Nirengeki Shoda, while Uraraka subdues Monoma, Yanagi and Kodai, completing Class 1-A's 4–0 win.

## Class 1-A as a Group

The class is defined less by its individual members than by the fact that **it is repeatedly attacked**. The U.S.J., the Forest Training Camp, the Bakugo kidnapping, the Kamino Ward incident, and eventually the Paranormal Liberation War all target Class 1-A specifically. Monoma's constant complaint in Class 1-B — that 1-A only receives attention because villains keep attacking them — is substantially correct, and the series lets him be right.

Being housed together in the **Heights Alliance** dorms from Season 3 onward turns them from a class into something closer to a family. The dorm-room competition in episode 52 (*"Create Those Ultimate Moves"*) is played for comedy, but it is also the point at which the series stops treating Class 1-A as a roster and starts treating it as a household.
