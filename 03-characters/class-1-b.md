## Leading Characters

### Itsuka Kendo (拳藤 一佳) — *Battle Fist*

**Quirk:** Big Fist · **Debut:** Season 2 — U.A. Sports Festival

Expands her hands and forearms to enormous size, granting tremendous striking power, reach, and the ability to shield others. She can also use her enlarged hands to catch projectiles and to carry people.

Kendo is Class 1-B's representative and its de facto leader — the closest thing the series has to a female counterpart to Tenya Iida, though considerably more relaxed about rules. She is sensible, direct, and physically capable of restraining **Neito Monoma** whenever he starts a fight with Class 1-A, which is often. She karate-chops the back of his neck and drags him away; it is one of the series' most reliable running gags.

She is one of the strongest hand-to-hand fighters among the first-years. In the Sports Festival she reaches the final tournament, and in Joint Training Round 2 she **leads the Class 1-B team to a 4–0 victory** over Yaoyorozu's team — the single most decisive win Class 1-B scores in the entire series, and the moment Monoma's claim that 1-B deserves respect stops being a joke.

She later fights in the Paranormal Liberation War and, in the epilogue, becomes a Pro Hero ranked in the national top ten alongside Monoma.

**Trivia.** She and Monoma have known each other since middle school, where she was already managing his behaviour.

### Neito Monoma (物間 寧人) — *Phantom Thief*

**Quirk:** Copy · **Debut:** Season 2 — U.A. Sports Festival

Copies any Quirk he touches. The limits are precise and important:

- He can hold up to **four** copied Quirks at once.
- Each copy lasts **five minutes**, extendable to about ten with training.
- **He copies the Quirk, not its accumulated resources.** A copied strength-enhancement Quirk gives him a baseline version, not the user's developed power. He cannot copy Fat Gum's absorption, because he does not have the body mass.

Monoma is the series' most committed agent of chaos. His entire personality is organised around an obsessive rivalry with Class 1-A, whom he considers arrogant for receiving more attention. He taunts them relentlessly, laughs maniacally, and is immediately subdued by Kendo.

**His backstory, revealed in the Joint Training arc,** explains the performance: as a child he was told he could not be a hero, because a Quirk that only works by borrowing other people's power means he would always be dependent on others. His antagonism toward Class 1-A is a defence mechanism — but his conviction that 1-B deserves equal recognition is sincere, and the narrative mostly agrees with him.

**Round 5 of the Joint Training.** Monoma's plan is to goad Midoriya, let **Shinso** brainwash him, and win the round. It nearly works. Instead, when Monoma grazes Midoriya and copies **One For All**, he discovers that the copy gives him almost nothing — the Quirk functions, but with none of its stockpiled power, because there has been no time to accumulate any.

What he learns instead is far more dangerous: **One For All is not a natural Quirk.** It is a stored legacy passed between people, which is why copying it yields an empty shell. Monoma keeps the deduction to himself, mentions it obliquely to Vlad King, and never betrays Midoriya — an act of loyalty that sits oddly against everything else about him.

By the end of the series he has become one of the most tactically important students in U.A., because Copy is the only Quirk that can replicate other people's abilities on demand. In the final arcs the heroes use him to move Quirks between fighters mid-battle. He reaches the **No. 10** position on the post-timeskip Hero Billboard Chart.

### Tetsutetsu Tetsutetsu (鉄哲 徹鐵) — *Real Steel*

**Quirk:** Steel · **Debut:** Season 2 — U.A. Sports Festival

Turns his entire body into steel. Simple, direct, and extremely effective in close combat — he can withstand explosions, blades and impacts that would kill an ordinary person.

Tetsutetsu is loud, hot-blooded, and devoted to the idea of manly confrontation, which makes him the natural rival and eventual close friend of **Eijiro Kirishima**. The two are deliberately written as parallels: same philosophy, same temperament, nearly identical combat approach, different classes — and, tellingly, the **same birthday**, October 16. Their Sports Festival first-round match ends in a draw because they knock each other out simultaneously.

His Quirk's weakness is heat and prolonged strain: steel softens, and he has a limited duration before his body gives out. He fills one of the two spots vacated when Ojiro and Shoda withdraw from the Sports Festival tournament on principle.

---

### Ibara Shiozaki (塩崎 茨) — *Vine*

**Quirk:** Vines · **Debut:** Season 2 — U.A. Sports Festival

Her hair is composed of living, thorn-covered vines she can extend, entangle, and use defensively. She can regrow them by consuming water and sunlight.

Shiozaki is devoutly religious and speaks in a formal, scriptural register, quoting scripture mid-fight. She is among the strongest fighters in Class 1-B: she places **fourth in the Sports Festival obstacle race**, ahead of Honenuki, and defeats Denki Kaminari in the tournament by simply grounding his electricity into the earth.

Her vines have considerable reach and can restrain multiple opponents simultaneously, but she is reluctant to use force and prefers to end conflicts without injury — which the series treats as a genuine strength rather than naivety. In Joint Training Round 1 she captures Kaminari, and is then brainwashed by Shinso, who mimics Hiryu Rin's voice to do it.

She fills the second of the two spots vacated by Ojiro and Shoda at the Sports Festival, after Kendo's team declines them.

---

### Juzo Honenuki (骨抜 柔造) — *Mudman*

**Quirk:** Softening · **Debut:** Season 2 — U.A. Sports Festival

Softens any non-living material he touches, turning ground, walls and metal into a quicksand-like substance — and can re-harden it at will. He cannot soften living tissue.

Honenuki is a calm, tactical fighter and one of Class 1-B's most strategically valuable members, because his Quirk controls the battlefield itself: he can trap opponents, create cover, or collapse structures. He entered U.A. by recommendation.

He leads the Class 1-B team in **Joint Training Round 3**, against Iida, Todoroki, Ojiro and Shoji. It is the only round of the five that ends in a **draw** — each team captures exactly one opponent before the time limit expires. Honenuki spends most of the match trying to take out Iida, eventually gives up when it becomes clear he cannot, and is caught anyway.

He is also an outstanding rescue specialist, since softening debris is one of the most useful Quirks imaginable for extracting trapped civilians.

## Remaining Students

### Kosei Tsuburaba (円場 硬化) — *Solid Air*
Exhales air that solidifies into walls and platforms. Their strength depends on his lung capacity, so he must keep breathing hard to keep them up. A defensive specialist who is often underestimated; his **Air Prison** can seal an opponent inside a box they cannot break out of, which in Round 1 of the Joint Training neutralises Koji Koda completely — a Quirk that requires speech is useless against someone sealed in solid air. He is one of the class's more outspoken members and frequently argues with Monoma.

### Sen Kaibara (回原 旋) — *Spiral*
Rotates any part of his body at high speed, including his whole torso and his fingers. The rotation generates drilling force, considerable momentum, and enough lift to hover briefly. A straightforward, energetic fighter who pairs well in team compositions and fights in Round 3.

### Kinoko Komori (小森 希乃子) — *Shemage*
Grows mushrooms from any surface she touches, and can release spores that grow inside an opponent's respiratory system once inhaled. Her Quirk is genuinely unsettling in combat and highly effective for area denial, blinding and tracking. She is quiet and slightly eerie, in deliberate contrast to her cheerful appearance. She fights in Round 2, on Kendo's winning team.

### Yui Kodai (小大 唯) — *Rule*
Alters the size of any non-living object she touches, within a total mass limit. She can shrink obstacles or enlarge tools and weapons to enormous scale. Kodai communicates almost entirely through short phrases and gestures and is extremely reserved — a Quirk called "Rule" belonging to a girl who barely speaks is one of the series' quieter jokes. Captured by Uraraka in Round 5.

### Nirengeki Shoda (庄田 二連撃) — *Mines*
Delivers a **double impact**: a second concussive force that strikes a target at range, after his first. It lets him hit from a distance without projectiles, and the second impact is stronger than the first. He is polite and thoughtful, one of Class 1-B's more analytical members. At the Sports Festival he is part of Team Shinso and **withdraws from the tournament alongside Ojiro**, refusing to advance on a result earned while brainwashed.

### Hiryu Rin (鱗 竜才) — *Long Weizi*
Grows and manipulates hard, sharp dragon-like scales across his body, which he can harden into armour or detach and fire as projectiles. A Chinese exchange student whose hero name is Chinese rather than Japanese. He fights in Round 1, where Shinso brainwashes Shiozaki by mimicking his voice.

### Kojiro Bondo (凡戸 固次郎) — *Plamo*
Secretes a strong adhesive substance — **Cemedine**, named after the Japanese glue brand — from his body. Effective for immobilising opponents and sealing areas. His appearance, a face resembling a glue tube with eye-like nozzles, makes him one of the class's most distinctive designs. Captured in Round 4.

### Togaru Kamakiri (鎌切 尖) — *Jack Mantis*
Produces sharp, retractable blades from his arms, shoulders and body, giving him a mantis-like fighting style. Aggressive and confident, he favours direct offence and is one of Class 1-B's strongest close-combat fighters. In Round 4 he is taken down by Bakugo.

### Setsuna Tokage (取蔭 切奈) — *Lizardy*
Splits her body into up to **fifty** independently controlled pieces, each of which she can move telekinetically and reattach. Extraordinarily versatile for reconnaissance, restraint and evasion — she can have eyes and hands in fifty places at once. She is blunt, teasing, and one of the class's sharpest tacticians, and she entered U.A. by recommendation. In Round 4 she is defeated by Sero with Bakugo's help.

### Jurota Shishida (獅子目 獣太) — *Gevaudan*
Transforms into a beast-like form with enhanced strength, speed and senses — and loses rational control while doing so. His signature technique, **Roaring Rage**, is a full-commitment charge. He speaks formally and behaves with old-fashioned courtesy, which makes the berserk transformation a deliberate contrast; his hero name references the Beast of Gévaudan.

He is Round 1's standout performer: he smashes both Kirishima and Asui through pipes in the opening seconds. He is then brainwashed by Shinso, who imitates Tsuburaba's voice using his Persona Cords, and is finally knocked out when Asui throws Hiryu Rin into his head.

### Shihai Kuroiro (黒色 支配) — *Vantablack*
Merges into and travels through darkness, emerging from any shadow. Extremely effective for stealth and ambush, but useless in bright light. He is theatrical and mischievous, and speaks in an affected villainous register that his classmates find exhausting.

His full hero name is **"Scheming Hero: Vantablack."** He is the direct counter to Fumikage Tokoyami: in Round 2 he fights a class containing Dark Shadow, and Class 1-B wins the round **4–0**. Darkness is Kuroiro's home and Tokoyami's hazard.

### Reiko Yanagi (柳 霊子) — *Emily*
Manipulates objects telekinetically within a weight limit of roughly two tons. Her pale appearance and deadpan delivery led classmates to compare her to a horror-film ghost, hence the hero name "Emily" — after the character from *The Ring*. She is quiet but formidable in team play, and in Round 5 she uses Poltergeist to throw metal pipes at Uraraka. Uraraka karate-chops her unconscious.

### Pony Tsunotori (角取 ポニー) — *Rocketti*
Grows and fires her horns as projectiles with homing capability, regrowing them afterwards. An American exchange student who speaks limited Japanese, which creates recurring comic misunderstandings. Her horns are strong enough to pierce concrete.

### Manga Fukidashi (吹出 漫我) — *Comicman*
Materialises onomatopoeia — he can draw sound-effect words into the air and make them physically real. "BOOM" explodes; "WIND" blows; "ICE" freezes. One of the most creatively potent Quirks in the series, limited mainly by his imagination, his drawing speed and his reaction time. He fights in Round 2 on Kendo's winning team.

### Yosetsu Awase (泡瀬 洋雪) — *Welder*
Fuses any two objects he touches together at a molecular level. He can weld metal, attach opponents to surfaces, join broken structures, or seal a door permanently. It is one of the most useful non-combat Quirks in U.A., and Awase is frequently the reason a rescue operation succeeds. He fights in Round 4 and is defeated by Kyoka Jiro.

## The Joint Training Result

Class 1-B loses the overall series, but the scoreline is closer than Monoma's complaining suggests:

| Round | Class 1-A team | Class 1-B team | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asui, Kirishima, Koda, Kaminari + Shinso | Shiozaki, Shishida, Tsuburaba, Rin | **1-A wins 4–2** |
| 2 | Aoyama, Yaoyorozu, Hagakure, Tokoyami | Kendo, Fukidashi, Komori, Kuroiro | **1-B wins 4–0** |
| 3 | Iida, Todoroki, Ojiro, Shoji | Honenuki, Tetsutetsu, Tsunotori, Kaibara | **Draw 1–1** |
| 4 | Bakugo, Sero, Jiro, Sato | Kamakiri, Tokage, Bondo, Awase | **1-A wins 4–0** |
| 5 | Midoriya, Uraraka, Ashido, Mineta | Monoma, Kodai, Shoda, Yanagi + Shinso | **1-A wins 4–0** |

**Overall: Class 1-A 3, Class 1-B 1, one draw.**

## Class 1-B as a Narrative Device

Class 1-B exists to make an argument. The series uses them to demonstrate that **Class 1-A's prominence is circumstantial, not intrinsic.** Monoma's constant complaint — that 1-A only gets attention because villains keep targeting them — is substantially correct, and the Joint Training arc proves that 1-B can win decisively when the conditions are even.

Horikoshi gives Class 1-B comparatively little screen time but uses it precisely: they are the control group that shows what a normal hero class looks like when it is not at the centre of a national crisis. Their lessons are stable, their teamwork is better developed, and their forethought is sharper — which is exactly why they lose to a class that has been traumatised into competence.

By the war arcs the two classes have to operate as a single unit, and Monoma's Copy becomes one of the heroes' most important tactical assets. The rivalry resolves not in victory but in mutual necessity.
