# Supporting Cast

*Civilians, other students, family members, and characters who shape the story from outside the hero and villain organisations.*

---

## Students Outside the Hero Course

### Hitoshi Shinso (心操 人使)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Brainwashing |
| **Birthday** | July 1 |
| **Course** | General Studies → Hero Course (from Year 2) |
| **Voice (JP)** | Wataru Hatano |
| **Voice (EN)** | Jarrod Greene |
| **Height** | 177 cm |
| **Debut** | Season 2, episode 15 — *"Roaring Sports Festival"* (manga chapter 23) |

Anyone who answers Shinso in conversation falls under his complete control until he releases them or they are physically disrupted. The Quirk is useless against an opponent who simply does not speak, and it leaves him defenceless in a straight fight.

**Why he matters.** Shinso is the series' clearest statement about how hero society judges Quirks. From childhood, people told him his power was **villainous** — that anyone who could control minds would inevitably become a criminal. He was placed in General Studies rather than the Hero Course despite wanting to be a hero, because no one would look past the Quirk. He is, in the series' own framing, what Midoriya would have become if All Might had never shown up.

At the Sports Festival he carries his cavalry team — Nirengeki Shoda, Yuga Aoyama and Mashirao Ojiro — to **third place** by brainwashing rival riders. Before the tournament draw, **Ojiro and Shoda both withdraw**, saying they cannot remember earning their place and refuse to advance on a result produced by someone else's control. Midnight accepts the withdrawals and puts Tetsutetsu and Shiozaki in the open slots.

In the tournament's first match Shinso faces **Midoriya**, who is losing badly until he breaks the brainwash by deliberately snapping his own fingers. Shinso is beaten, and in his exit interview he tells the watching teachers exactly what he thinks of a system that sorts children by Quirk before it ever asks what they want. He is not punished. He is simply refused.

**Development.** Aizawa takes Shinso on as a personal protégé. Because Brainwashing is useless against a silent opponent, Shinso trains in hand-to-hand combat and learns to use Aizawa's **binding cloth**. **Mei Hatsume** builds him an **Artificial Vocal Cords** mask — also called Persona Cords — which lets him imitate other people's voices, and therefore brainwash people who would never answer *him*.

In the **Joint Training Battle** he fights for both classes — one team each — so that Aizawa and Vlad King can assess him under identical conditions. He brainwashes Shishida in Round 1 by mimicking Tsuburaba's voice, and brainwashes Shiozaki by mimicking Rin's. In Round 5 he brainwashes Midoriya to shut down a Blackwhip outbreak that would otherwise have injured everyone on the field; Midoriya then breaks free, defeats him, and Class 1-A takes the round 4–0.

Shinso captures nobody and loses the rematch, and tells the teachers he is disappointed in himself. **Vlad King announces that he will join the Hero Course from his second year** — the only student in the series to transfer in mid-course.

**Trivia.** He is deliberately designed to resemble Aizawa — same hair, same tired expression, same binding cloth, same fighting style — which the narrative treats as both coincidence and inheritance.

### Mei Hatsume (発目 明)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Zoom |
| **Birthday** | April 18 |
| **Course** | Support Course, U.A. |
| **Voice (JP)** | Shiori Sakurai |
| **Voice (EN)** | Alexis Tipton |
| **Debut** | **Season 2, episode 15** — *"Roaring Sports Festival"* (manga chapter 25) |

A Support Course student and inventor of extraordinary talent and zero social restraint. She refers to her inventions as her "**babies**" and describes them in loving, unsettling detail, generally while standing far too close to whoever she is talking to.

Her debut is often misremembered as Season 3, because that is when she becomes a recurring character. She first appears in the Season 2 obstacle race, and attaches herself to Midoriya's team in the cavalry battle — the only team that will have her — for the express purpose of advertising her equipment on national television. Midoriya finishes fourth largely because her hover soles work.

In the Sports Festival tournament she faces Tenya Iida and **deliberately showcases every piece of her gear** before conceding, treating the match as a trade show rather than a competition. It is one of the funniest sequences in the series and also a completely rational career move.

Her Zoom Quirk lets her see objects in extreme detail at distance — ideal for engineering, useless for combat. She builds and maintains much of Class 1-A's support equipment, including Shinso's Persona Cords. She has no interest in being a hero and no interest in being anything except an inventor, which makes her one of the few U.A. students entirely free of the series' central anxieties.

### The Structure of U.A.

U.A. is divided into four courses: **Hero**, **Support**, **General Studies** and **Management**. The series uses this structure to make a pointed argument — that U.A.'s Hero Course selects for combat ability, which means talented people with non-combat Quirks are filtered out and then told they were never heroes to begin with. Shinso is the emblem of this. Hatsume and the Support Course students are the counter-example: they are essential, and the ranking system has no category for them. The Management Course students who run the School Festival are treated with equal seriousness.

## Civilians and Family

### Eri (エリ)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Rewind |
| **Birthday** | December 21 |
| **Age** | 6 |
| **Height** | 110 cm |
| **Voice (JP)** | Seiran Kobayashi |
| **Voice (EN)** | Emily Neves |
| **Debut** | Season 3, episode 63 |

A small girl descended from the bloodline of the **Shie Hassaikai** boss — she is his granddaughter. Her Quirk, **Rewind**, reverses a living body to a previous state. It is so powerful that, uncontrolled, it can erase a person from existence entirely.

**Her abuse.** Eri accidentally rewound her own father out of existence as a toddler. **Overhaul** discovered what she could do, told her she was cursed, that her existence hurt people, and that she should not have been born — and then **murdered her repeatedly and rewound her body** to harvest her cells for Quirk-Destroying Bullets.

She escaped once. She ran into Midoriya in the street and begged him to put her back, because she believed she deserved to go back. Midoriya let Overhaul take her. He does not forgive himself for it, and neither does the narrative.

**The rescue.** During the raid, Eri screams and activates Rewind on Midoriya as he is being destroyed by his own power at 100% Full Cowl. Her Rewind undoes his injuries as fast as he inflicts them, which is what allows him to defeat Overhaul. She is placed in U.A.'s care afterward, with a device on her horn to suppress Rewind, and slowly begins to recover: she smiles, she laughs, she asks to attend the School Festival. Her first genuine smile is one of the most emotionally effective moments in Season 4.

⚠️ **Later.** Eri eventually masters Rewind well enough to restore **Mirio Togata's** lost Quirk — closing the arc that began with his sacrifice for her. She is also, structurally, the most important non-combatant in the series: her Quirk is the only thing in the world that can undo what All For One does.

### Kota Izumi (出水 洸汰)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Birthday** | December 12 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Michiru Yamazaki |
| **Voice (EN)** | Cassandra Lee Morris |

A boy Midoriya meets during the Forest Training Camp. Kota's parents were both Pro Heroes — the duo **Water Hose** — who died in the line of duty saving strangers. He therefore despises hero society: he considers heroes to be arrogant people who get themselves killed for applause and leave children behind.

**Mandalay is his aunt**, and becomes his guardian. She is one of the Wild, Wild Pussycats running the camp, which makes her presence there quietly devastating — she is raising the child her siblings' deaths created, and she works in the profession that killed them.

Midoriya, rather than argue, tells Kota plainly that he does not need to like heroes. When **Muscular** — the villain who murdered Kota's parents — attacks him, Midoriya throws himself in front of the blow and pushes One For All past every limit he has ever used, to **1,000,000%**, shattering both arms completely.

Episode 42, *"My Hero,"* is one of the series' finest single episodes, and Kota's change of heart is earned entirely through action rather than dialogue. ⚠️ He appears again in the epilogue, as a U.A. student himself.

### Inko Midoriya (緑谷 引子)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Attract Small |
| **Birthday** | July 4 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Aya Kawakami |
| **Voice (EN)** | Jessica Cavanagh |

Midoriya's mother. Her Quirk lets her attract small objects toward herself. Combined with his father's fire-breathing Quirk, it is the source of Midoriya's own analysis of how Quirks combine across generations — and of his mother's tearful apology in episode 1, when she tells him she is sorry he was born without one.

Inko is the series' most sympathetic adult. She loves her son completely and is frightened for him constantly. Her worry is not presented as an obstacle to his dream but as a **reasonable response to it**: her son is repeatedly hospitalised, is attacked by villains twice in his first year, and is being trained by a man she has never met to inherit a power that visibly destroys his body.

Midoriya keeps the truth from her for almost the entire series. When she finally learns what he has been doing, her reaction is terror and grief rather than pride — and the narrative treats her as correct.

**Hisashi Midoriya**, Izuku's father, is a fire-breathing Quirk holder who works overseas and is essentially absent from the story. Horikoshi has stated he exists mainly to account for Izuku's Quirk genetics.

The Todoroki household is the series' central case study in inherited harm. Endeavor built Shoto deliberately, destroyed Toya accidentally, and drove Rei insane. Horikoshi never allows the family to reconcile fully — the damage is permanent, and the best available outcome is that they stop making it worse.

Season 5's Christmas dinner is the family's first meal together in years. Nobody forgives anybody. Everybody stays at the table, which is presented as progress.

### The Iida Family

**Tensei Iida / Ingenium** (born July 22) — Tenya's older brother, a Pro Hero crippled permanently by the Hero Killer Stain. He survives but can no longer walk or work as a hero. Tenya's entire Season 2 arc is driven by grief and rage over this, and by the fact that his brother never wanted revenge.

Tensei is warm where Tenya is rigid, and the series uses the contrast to show what Tenya is at risk of becoming. Tenya eventually inherits the Ingenium name — but chooses it deliberately, on his own terms, rather than receiving it.

### Oboro Shirakumo (白雲 朧)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hero name** | Loud Cloud |
| **Quirk** | Cloud |
| **Birthday** | May 5 |
| **Height** | 187 cm |
| **Age at death** | 17 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Kensho Ono |
| **Voice (EN)** | Stephen Sanders |
| **Debut** | Season 5, episode 107 (manga chapter 255) |

A U.A. student in the same year as **Shota Aizawa** and **Hizashi Yamada**, and their close friend. His Cloud Quirk let him generate and control masses of cloud, and he wanted nothing more than to open his own hero agency with the two of them. He was killed in a villain attack while still a student.

⚠️ All For One recovered his body, and Doctor Garaki rebuilt it into **Kurogiri**, the League of Villains' warp-gate Nomu. Aizawa and Yamada spend their entire adult careers not knowing this. Every student they lost at the U.S.J. and the Forest Training Camp was transported by their dead friend's body.

The discovery, in Season 5 episode 107, is one of the most emotionally devastating sequences in the series — and it exists to give Aizawa a personal reason to fight in the war that follows.

## Detectives and Officials

### Naomasa Tsukauchi (塚内 直正) — *True Man*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Polygraph |
| **Birthday** | April 4 |
| **Voice (JP)** | Tokuyoshi Kawashima |
| **Voice (EN)** | Alejandro Saab |

A police detective whose Quirk lets him detect lies with perfect accuracy — he can tell instantly whether a statement is true, false, or something the speaker believes but is mistaken about. The Quirk's cost is that he cannot lie either, which makes him bad at small talk and excellent at his job.

He is All Might's closest friend among the authorities and one of the very few people who knows the truth about One For All from early in the series. He functions as the story's connection to normal law enforcement — and as a reminder that heroes operate within a legal system that does not always approve of them. It is Tsukauchi who arranges the cover-up of the Hosu Incident, and who explains to Midoriya why three unlicensed students cannot be prosecuted for saving a life.

## One For All's Previous Users

The **vestiges** — personality imprints stored inside One For All — become accessible to Midoriya from Season 5 onward. Each contributed a Quirk, and each Quirk is stronger in Midoriya's hands than it was in theirs.

| # | User | Quirk contributed | Voice (JP) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | **Yoichi Shigaraki** | The stockpile that became One For All | Soichiro Hoshi | All For One's younger brother. Born apparently Quirkless; given a stockpiling Quirk by his brother as an act of contempt, which he then combined with his own will to create One For All. |
| **2nd** | Unnamed | **Gearshift** | Daisuke Ono | Alters the speed and trajectory of whatever it touches |
| **3rd** | Unnamed | **Fa Jin** | Ryota Suzuki | Stores kinetic energy from repeated movement and releases it explosively |
| **4th** | **Hikage Shinomori** | **Danger Sense** | Toshiyuki Morikawa | Detects hostile intent. Spent his life hiding in the mountains to avoid All For One, and died of old age without ever fighting him. |
| **5th** | **Daigoro Banjo** | **Blackwhip** | Hiroki Yasumoto | Dark energy tendrils controlled by willpower; the first vestige Midoriya accesses |
| **6th** | **En** | **Smokescreen** | Tetsuya Kakihara | Generates obscuring smoke |
| **7th** | **Nana Shimura** | **Float** | Mie Sonozaki | All Might's mentor. Abandoned her family to protect them from All For One — which is how her grandson became Tomura Shigaraki. |
| **8th** | **Toshinori Yagi** (All Might) | — | Kenta Miyake | Born Quirkless. Contributed nothing but the stockpile, which is why One For All grew so much larger in his hands. |
| **9th** | **Izuku Midoriya** | — | Daiki Yamashita | The current wielder. |

**Hikage Shinomori** is the series' quiet tragedy: a man who correctly understood that he could not beat All For One, hid for his entire life to preserve One For All for someone else, and is remembered by the other vestiges as a coward. His Danger Sense turns out to be one of Midoriya's most useful Quirks.

**Nana Shimura** is the reason the series exists. Her decision to protect her son by abandoning him produced both All Might — whom she trained — and Tomura Shigaraki, whom her abandonment produced. The two opposing forces of the entire story originate in one woman's attempt to keep her child safe.
