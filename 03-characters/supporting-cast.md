# Supporting Cast

*Civilians, other students, family members, and characters who shape the story from outside the hero and villain organisations.*

---

## Students Outside Class 1-A and 1-B

### Hitoshi Shinso (心操 人使) — *Nighthide*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Brainwashing |
| **Course** | General Studies (transfers to Hero Course) |
| **Voice (JP)** | Wataru Hatano |
| **Voice (EN)** | Jarrod Greene |
| **Debut** | Season 2 (Sports Festival) |

Anyone who answers Shinso in conversation falls under his complete control until he releases them or is physically disrupted.

**Why he matters.** Shinso is the series' clearest statement about how hero society judges Quirks. From childhood, people told him his power was **villainous** — that anyone who could control minds would inevitably become a criminal. He was placed in General Studies rather than the Hero Course despite wanting to be a hero, because no one would look past the Quirk.

At the Sports Festival he tells Midoriya directly that he will become a hero specifically to spite everyone who told him he could not. Midoriya — who was told the same thing about being Quirkless — beats him, but the encounter changes both of them.

**Development.** Aizawa takes Shinso on as a personal protégé. Because Brainwashing is useless against an opponent who does not speak, Shinso trains in hand-to-hand combat and learns to use Aizawa's **capture weapon**. Mei Hatsume builds him a voice-modulating mask that lets him disguise his voice and provoke responses.

During the **Joint Training Arc** in Season 5, he fights alongside Class 1-A as a trial and performs well enough to earn his transfer into the Hero Course. He is one of the most satisfying slow-burn developments in the series.

**Trivia.** He is deliberately designed to resemble Aizawa — same hair, same tired expression, same fighting style — which the narrative treats as both coincidence and inheritance.

### Mei Hatsume (発目 明)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Zoom (enhanced vision) |
| **Course** | Support Course, U.A. |
| **Voice (JP)** | Azu Sakura |
| **Voice (EN)** | Alexis Tipton |
| **Debut** | Season 3 |

A Support Course student and inventor of extraordinary talent and zero social restraint. She refers to her inventions as her "**babies**" and describes them in loving, unsettling detail.

Mei builds and maintains much of Class 1-A's equipment: Midoriya's Iron Soles and later costumes, Shinso's voice mask, and numerous support items for other students. She is the reason the series can introduce new gear without breaking its internal logic.

Her Zoom Quirk lets her see objects in extreme detail at distance — useful for engineering, useless for combat. She has no interest in being a hero and no interest in being anything except an inventor, which makes her one of the few U.A. students completely free of the series' central anxieties.

---

### The General Studies and Support Courses

U.A. is divided into four courses: **Hero**, **Support**, **General Studies**, and **Management**. The series uses this structure to make a pointed argument — that U.A.'s Hero Course selects for combat ability, which means talented people with non-combat Quirks are filtered out and then told they were never heroes to begin with. Shinso is the emblem of this, but the Management Course students who run the School Festival are treated with equal seriousness.

## Civilians and Family

### Eri (エリ)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Quirk** | Rewind |
| **Voice (JP)** | Seiran Kobayashi |
| **Voice (EN)** | Emily Neves |
| **Debut** | Season 4 |

A small girl descended from the yakuza bloodline of the Shie Hassaikai. Her Quirk, **Rewind**, reverses a living body to a previous state. It is so powerful that, uncontrolled, it can erase a person from existence.

**Her abuse.** **Overhaul** discovered her Quirk when she accidentally rewound her own father out of existence. He told her that she was cursed, that her existence hurt people, and that she should not have been born. He then **murdered her repeatedly and rewound her body** to harvest her cells for Quirk-Destroying Bullets.

She is the series' most important non-combatant. Everything about the Shie Hassaikai arc is organised around her — and the narrative is explicit that the heroes were too late for years of her suffering.

**The rescue.** During the raid, Eri screams and activates Rewind on Midoriya as he is about to die, healing his destroyed body. She is placed in a hospital afterward and slowly begins to recover: she smiles, she laughs, she asks to attend the School Festival. Her first genuine smile is one of the most emotionally effective moments in Season 4.

**Later.** Eri eventually masters Rewind well enough to restore **Mirio Togata's** lost Quirk — closing the arc that began with his sacrifice for her.

### Kota Izumi (出水 洸汰)

**Voice (JP):** Michiru Yamazaki · **Voice (EN):** Cassandra Lee Morris

A boy Midoriya meets during the Forest Training Camp. Kota's parents were both Pro Heroes who died in the line of duty, and he therefore despises hero society — he considers heroes to be arrogant people who get themselves killed for applause.

Midoriya, rather than argue, tells him plainly that he does not need to like heroes. When **Muscular** — the villain who murdered Kota's parents — attacks him, Midoriya throws himself in front of the blow and shatters both arms to 1,000,000% to save him.

Episode 42, "My Hero," is one of the series' finest single episodes, and Kota's change of heart is earned entirely through action rather than dialogue. He appears again years later, in the epilogue, as a U.A. student himself.

---

### Inko Midoriya (緑谷 引子)

**Voice (JP):** Aya Kawakami · **Voice (EN):** Jessica Cavanagh

Midoriya's mother. Her Quirk lets her attract small objects toward her — the source of Midoriya's own analysis of how Quirks combine across generations.

Inko is the series' most sympathetic adult. She loves her son completely and is frightened for him constantly. When she learns he has inherited All Might's power, her reaction is not pride but terror — she asks him, on camera, whether he is going to die.

Her quiet grief is one of the reasons Midoriya's self-destructiveness is treated as a problem rather than a virtue.

### The Todoroki Family

| Member | Relationship | Voice (JP) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rei Todoroki** | Endeavor's wife | Michiko Neya | Driven to a breakdown by his abuse; poured boiling water on Shoto's face; hospitalised for years |
| **Toya Todoroki** | Eldest son | Hiro Shimono (as Dabi) | Presumed dead in a fire; became **Dabi** |
| **Fuyumi Todoroki** | Daughter | Kei Shindo | A teacher; the family's peacemaker; organises the disastrous family dinner in Season 5 |
| **Natsuo Todoroki** | Younger son | Yuuki Shin | Refuses to speak to Endeavor; the family's most openly angry member |
| **Shoto Todoroki** | Youngest son | Yuki Kaji | See [Class 1-A](class-1-a.md) |

The Todoroki household is the series' central case study in inherited harm. Endeavor built Shoto deliberately, destroyed Toya accidentally, and drove Rei insane. Horikoshi never allows the family to reconcile fully — the damage is permanent, and the best available outcome is that they stop making it worse.

### Tenya Iida's Family

**Tensei Iida / Ingenium** — Tenya's older brother, a Pro Hero crippled permanently by Stain. He survives but can no longer walk or work as a hero. Tenya's entire Season 2 arc is driven by grief and rage over this.

## Detectives and Officials

### Naomasa Tsukauchi (塚内 直正) — *True Man*

**Quirk:** Polygraph · **Voice (JP):** Tokuyoshi Kawashima · **Voice (EN):** Alejandro Saab

A police detective whose Quirk lets him detect lies with perfect accuracy. He is All Might's closest friend among the authorities and one of the few people who knows the truth about One For All from the beginning.

Tsukauchi is warm, dry, and completely unimpressed by All Might's theatrics. He functions as the series' connection to normal law enforcement — and as a reminder that heroes operate within a legal system that does not always approve of them.
