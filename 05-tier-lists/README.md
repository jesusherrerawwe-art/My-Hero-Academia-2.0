# Power Tier Lists: Methodology

*How the rankings in this section were constructed.*

---

## ⚠️ Spoiler Warning

These tier lists rank characters by their **peak power across the entire series**, including the manga's final arcs, the anime's final season, and the epilogue. They contain **major spoilers for events well beyond Season 5**, including character deaths, Quirk awakenings, and the identity of the U.A. traitor.

If you want to avoid spoilers, use the [Seasons](../02-seasons/README.md) and [Characters](../03-characters/README.md) sections instead — both stay largely within Seasons 1–5 and flag anything later inline.

---

## What Is Being Ranked

Each character is ranked at the **strongest they ever become** at any point in the story — not their condition during Seasons 1–5, and not their condition at the end.

| Character | Ranked as | Not ranked as |
|---|---|---|
| **Izuku Midoriya** | One For All at 100% with all six inherited Quirks | The bone-breaking 100% of Season 1 |
| **Tomura Shigaraki** | Awakened, with All For One installed | The petulant leader of the U.S.J. attack |
| **Endeavor** | *PLUS ULTRA Prominence Burn* against the High-End Hood | His Season 2 appearance |
| **Mirio Togata** | Permeation restored by Eri's Rewind | The Quirkless year he spends as a spectator |
| **Ragdoll** | Search, before All For One steals it | The version of her that exists afterwards |
| **Ochaco Uraraka** | Zero Gravity after its awakening | The girl who could only lift one thing at a time |

**Characters who die are still ranked.** Death is not a demotion; the list measures what a character was capable of at their maximum, and several of the highest-ranked people on these pages do not survive the series.

---

## Criteria

Characters are assessed on five factors, in rough order of weight:

| Factor | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Destructive ceiling** | The single largest amount of damage the character can produce at their peak |
| **Durability / survivability** | How much punishment they can take and keep fighting |
| **Versatility** | Whether the Quirk solves problems beyond raw combat |
| **Combat skill** | Technique, tactical intelligence and experience, independent of the Quirk |
| **Consistency** | Whether peak output is *reliably reachable*, or requires conditions that rarely occur |

**Consistency is the factor that does the most work.** A character with an enormous ceiling but poor control ranks below a slightly weaker character who can reliably reach their maximum. This is why **Denki Kaminari** sits in C despite a Quirk that could theoretically flatten a city block, why **Shuichi Iguchi (Spinner)** sits in B despite a borrowed Final War form with A-tier output, and why **Tamaki Amajiki** is at the top of B rather than in A.

---

## What Is *Not* Being Ranked

These lists measure **combat power**. They deliberately exclude:

- **Narrative importance.** Sir Nighteye is essential to the plot and ranks in B.
- **Moral standing.** Several villains outrank every hero on the page.
- **Team usefulness.** A support specialist can be invaluable and still rank in C.
- **Intelligence.** Principal Nezu is arguably the smartest character in the series and sits in D for combat.
- **Heroic impact.** All Might changed the world more than anyone; his ranking reflects his fists, not his legacy.
- **What a character builds.** Doctor Garaki personally ranks C. Every Nomu in the series — including Gigantomachia and Hood — is his work.

---

## Scope and Exclusions

| Included | Excluded |
|---|---|
| Named characters with a defined Quirk | Anonymous civilians |
| Heroes, students, staff and villains | Mass-produced and unnamed **Nomu** — engineered creatures, not characters |
| Characters from all eight seasons and the full manga, including the epilogue | The named High-End Nomu **Hood** — same reason |
| **Gigantomachia**, as a named individual with a will and a history | Film-only characters (*Heroes: Rising*'s Nine, *World Heroes' Mission*'s Flect Turn and Rody Soul, *Two Heroes*' Wolfram) |
| Other hero schools: Shiketsu, Ketsubutsu | Spin-off-only characters (*Vigilantes*' Koichi Haimawari, Knuckleduster, Number 6) |
| Historical figures the series actually shows fighting (Nana Shimura) | Historical figures it never shows fighting (Destro) |
| | **One For All's vestiges** — they fight in the final war, but as manifestations of stored personality inside Midoriya, and are already counted in his ranking |

Each tier list also carries its own **"Not Ranked"** table at the end, listing specific characters excluded and why.

## Tier Definitions

Both lists use the same six tiers. The subtitle differs slightly between them because the top of the girls' list is defined by reality-altering abilities rather than raw destruction.

| Tier | Boys list | Girls list | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S+** | Existential | Reality-Altering | Capable of ending a city, a country, or the existing social order alone. Cannot be countered by conventional means. |
| **S** | Nation-Level | Catastrophic | Among the strongest beings in the world. Requires multiple top-tier combatants to defeat. |
| **A** | Elite | Elite | Top-ranked Pro Heroes, the strongest students, and major villain executives. |
| **B** | Strong | Strong | Competent licensed heroes, well-developed students, and dangerous specialist villains. |
| **C** | Capable | Capable | Solid fighters whose Quirks have real combat application but limited ceilings. |
| **D** | Non-Combatant | Non-Combatant | Support, medical, intellectual or civilian characters not built for direct conflict. |

**One special case.** **Eri** is placed in S+ on the girls' list as an explicit **ceiling ranking, not a combat ranking.** Rewind has the highest theoretical maximum of any Quirk in the series — it can erase a person from existence — but Eri is a six-year-old who cannot control it and who loses to almost everyone on the page in any conventional fight. The entry says so in full.

## A Note on Fairness

Power scaling in *My Hero Academia* is deliberately unstable. Quirks interact unpredictably: **Erasure** nullifies almost anyone, **Brainwashing** defeats anyone who speaks, **Compress** can capture someone far stronger, **Black Hole** disintegrates matter regardless of durability, and **Mushroom** can grow inside a throat and shut down a living shadow.

A tier list flattens those interactions, which is a real limitation of the format.

**Shota Aizawa ranks B and survives fights against S-tier opponents routinely**, because his Quirk removes the variable that makes them S-tier. **Neito Monoma ranks C** and is one of the most tactically valuable students at U.A., because Copy gives him other people's Quirks without other people's accumulated power. **Hitoshi Shinso ranks B** with the lowest raw output of anyone in that tier.

The rankings should be read as a measure of **direct confrontational power**, not as a prediction of who wins any given fight. That caveat is worth stating out loud in a school project: the gap between "how strong is this character" and "who wins this fight" is the single most interesting thing about how Horikoshi writes combat, and no tier list can capture it.

## Sources

Rankings are grounded in feats stated or shown in the manga and anime, cross-checked against:

- *My Hero Academia* manga, chapters 1–431 (2014–2024)
- *My Hero Academia* anime, episodes 1–171 (2016–2026)
- Official databooks: *Ultra Archive*, *Ultra Analysis* (character stat sheets), and the per-volume character profiles
- Fandom battle pages for individual encounter outcomes (U.S.J., Kamino, Shie Hassaikai, Paranormal Liberation War, Final War)

Where the databooks give numeric stats — Power, Speed, Technique, Intelligence, Cooperativeness — those inform the **combat skill** and **consistency** factors but do not override depicted feats.
