# Power Tier Lists: Methodology

*How the rankings in this section were constructed.*

---

## ⚠️ Spoiler Warning

These tier lists rank characters by their **peak power across the entire series**, including the manga's final arcs and the anime's final season. This means they contain **major spoilers for events well beyond Season 5**.

If you want to avoid spoilers, use the [Seasons](../02-seasons/README.md) and [Characters](../03-characters/README.md) sections instead.

---

## What Is Being Ranked

Each character is ranked at the **strongest they ever become** at any point in the story — not their condition during Seasons 1–5.

For example:
- **Izuku Midoriya** is ranked with full access to all six inherited One For All Quirks, not the bone-breaking 100% of Season 1.
- **Tomura Shigaraki** is ranked in his awakened form, not the petulant leader of the U.S.J. attack.
- **Endeavor** is ranked at his peak against a High-End Nomu, not his Season 2 appearance.
- **Mirio Togata** is ranked with Permeation restored, not during the period he was Quirkless.

---

## Criteria

Characters are assessed on five factors, in rough order of weight:

| Factor | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Destructive ceiling** | The single largest amount of damage the character can produce at their peak |
| **Durability / survivability** | How much punishment they can take and keep fighting |
| **Versatility** | Whether the Quirk solves problems beyond raw combat |
| **Combat skill** | Technique, tactical intelligence, and experience independent of the Quirk |
| **Consistency** | Whether peak output is reliably reachable, or requires conditions that rarely occur |

A character with an enormous ceiling but poor control ranks below a slightly weaker character who can reliably reach their maximum.

---

## What Is *Not* Being Ranked

These lists measure **combat power**. They deliberately exclude:

- **Narrative importance.** Sir Nighteye is essential to the plot and ranks low here.
- **Moral standing.** Several villains outrank every hero.
- **Team usefulness.** A support specialist can be invaluable and still rank in C.
- **Intelligence.** Principal Nezu is arguably the smartest character in the series and is placed in D for combat.
- **Heroic impact.** All Might changed the world more than anyone; his ranking reflects his fists, not his legacy.

---

## Scope and Exclusions

| Included | Excluded |
|---|---|
| Named characters with a defined Quirk | Generic **Nomu** (engineered creatures, not characters) |
| Heroes, students, staff, and villains | Anonymous civilians |
| Characters from all eight seasons and the full manga | Characters appearing only in films (*Heroes Rising*'s Nine, *World Heroes' Mission*'s Rody Soul) unless noted |
| **Gigantomachia** (a named individual) | Mass-produced Nomu |

## Tier Definitions

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **S+** | **Existential.** Capable of ending a city, a country, or the existing social order alone. Cannot be countered by conventional means. |
| **S** | **Nation-level.** Among the strongest beings in the world. Requires multiple top-tier combatants to defeat. |
| **A** | **Elite.** Top-ranked Pro Heroes, the strongest students, and major villain executives. |
| **B** | **Strong.** Competent licensed heroes, well-developed students, and dangerous specialist villains. |
| **C** | **Capable.** Solid fighters whose Quirks have real combat application but limited ceilings. |
| **D** | **Non-combatant.** Support, medical, intellectual, or civilian characters not built for direct conflict. |

---

## A Note on Fairness

Power scaling in *My Hero Academia* is deliberately unstable. Quirks interact unpredictably — **Erasure** nullifies almost anyone, **Brainwashing** defeats anyone who speaks, **Compress** can capture someone far stronger, and **Black Hole** disintegrates matter regardless of durability.

A tier list flattens these interactions, which is a real limitation. **Aizawa ranks B but defeats S-tier opponents routinely**, because his Quirk removes the variable that makes them S-tier. The rankings below should be read as a measure of **direct confrontational power**, not as a prediction of who wins any given fight.

That caveat is worth stating in a school project: it is the single most interesting thing about how Horikoshi writes combat.
