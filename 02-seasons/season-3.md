# Season 3 (2018)

*"Next... it's your turn."*

---

## Season Information

| | |
|---|---|
| **Episodes** | 25 (numbered 39–63) |

| **Manga chapters adapted** | 70–122 |
| **Arcs covered** | Forest Training Camp · Hideout Raid (Kamino Ward) · Provisional Hero License Exam · Shie Hassaikai (opening) |

Season 3 is the turning point of the entire series. It contains the event that restructures everything that follows: **All Might's final battle and retirement**.

---

## Arc-by-Arc Breakdown

### 1. Forest Training Camp Arc — Episodes 39–45

**What happens:** Following summer break, Class 1-A travels to a mountain facility for intensive Quirk training supervised by the Pro Hero team **Wild, Wild Pussycats** — Mandalay, Pixie-Bob, Ragdoll, and Tiger.

The training is designed around the principle of pushing a Quirk past its normal limits. Bakugo is forced to expand his explosions until his arms give out; Todoroki alternates between fire and ice until he loses feeling; Midoriya throws himself off a cliff repeatedly to build tolerance for One For All.

**Key moments:**
- Midoriya befriends **Kota Izumi**, a boy whose parents were Pro Heroes killed in the line of duty and who therefore despises hero society. When the villain **Muscular** attacks Kota, Midoriya intervenes — shattering both arms to 1,000,000% and defeating him. Kota's view of heroes begins to change.
- The **Vanguard Action Squad**, a League of Villains strike team consisting of **Dabi**, **Himiko Toga**, **Twice**, **Moonfish**, and **Mustard**, attacks the camp.
- **Ragdoll's Search Quirk is stolen** by All For One's agent — the first on-screen confirmation that Quirks can be taken.
- Mustard floods the facility with sleeping gas; Momo Yaoyorozu creates gas masks for both Class 1-A and Class 1-B.
- **Katsuki Bakugo is kidnapped.** The League wanted him alive because Shigaraki believed he could be turned to villainy.
- Aizawa fights Moonfish to protect his students and is critically injured, losing an eye.
- The season's darkest note: despite a successful defence, U.A. failed to protect a student. Public confidence in the school collapses.

**Characters introduced:** Mandalay, Pixie-Bob, Ragdoll, Tiger, Kota Izumi, Muscular, Moonfish, Mustard, Dabi, Himiko Toga, Twice.

---

### 2. Hideout Raid Arc / Kamino Ward — Episodes 46–49

**What happens:** Bakugo is held at the League's hideout in Kamino Ward. Rather than wait for an official rescue operation, Midoriya, Iida, Todoroki, Kirishima, and Yaoyorozu go in themselves — a decision the series treats as reckless, not heroic.

Meanwhile, **All For One** breaks out of Tartarus prison to retrieve Bakugo and to finally kill All Might in public.

**Key moments:**

**The rescue.** Kirishima reaches Bakugo, who is unharmed but furious at being treated as a victim. Shigaraki attempts to decay Midoriya, who has disguised himself as a wall of debris to enter the hideout. The students escape with Bakugo — but their actions nearly get them expelled, and they draw enormous public criticism.

**All Might vs. All For One.** The most important fight in the series, broadcast live across Japan.

- All For One, who has lived over a century by stealing Quirks, unleashes dozens of stolen abilities at once.
- All Might is already operating past his three-hour limit. During the fight, his remaining time runs out entirely.
- He wins with a single, definitive attack — **United States of Smash** — powered by the last ember of One For All.
- As All For One is carried away, All Might stands before the cameras and raises a finger, pointing directly at the lens: **"Next... it's your turn."**
- His muscle form collapses. The world sees the Symbol of Peace as a skeletal, dying man.

**The aftermath — the true catastrophe:**
- All Might **retires**. One For All passes fully to Midoriya, who can now access it at any time.
- All For One is imprisoned in Tartarus, but he is *smiling*. He tells Shigaraki — who is watching on television — that the Symbol of Peace is gone.
- **Crime rates surge.** Without a deterrent, villains organise openly. The Meta Liberation Army, the yakuza, and the remnants of the League all begin to move.
- All Might gives Midoriya his old costume's gauntlets and the two begin training seriously.

This is the moment the series stops being an adventure and becomes a tragedy about institutional collapse.

### 3. Provisional Hero License Exam Arc — Episodes 50–62

**What happens:** In response to the kidnapping, U.A. converts to a **boarding school**. Class 1-A moves into **Heights Alliance**, and the students' daily lives become a shared, supervised existence — a decision that deepens the class bond considerably.

The class then trains to develop **ultimate moves** (with support-course student **Mei Hatsume** building Midoriya's new Iron Soles costume), and takes the **Provisional Hero License Exam** at the National Dagobah Arena.

**The exam structure:**
1. **Round one — Elimination.** Hundreds of examinees compete in a free-for-all to place balls on each other's targets. Only 100 advance.
2. **Round two — Rescue.** Examinees must evacuate civilians from a simulated disaster while "HUC" (Help Us Company) role-players judge their conduct.

**Key moments:**
- **Shiketsu High** is the main rival school, represented by **Inasa Yoarashi** (Whirlwind), **Yo Shindo** (Vibrate), **Seiji Shishikura** (Meatball), and **Utsushimi Camie** (Glamour).
- **Inasa Yoarashi** is one of the series' most interesting supporting characters: a boy who idolised Endeavor so completely that he learned to hate him, and who is disqualified from the exam because he cannot separate his personal feelings from his duty.
- **Nagamasa** — actually **Ms. Joke** of Ketsubutsu Academy — and her class provide comic and tactical opposition.
- **Hound Dog** and **Gang Orca** serve as examiners; Gang Orca's presence intimidates the students into real fear.
- Midoriya and Bakugo have a brutal, ugly confrontation in the exam's aftermath (**"Deku vs. Kacchan, Part 2"**) in which Bakugo finally forces Midoriya to explain One For All. Midoriya tells him the truth — again. Bakugo still does not fully believe it, but the two reconcile enough to stop being enemies.
- **All Might takes Midoriya to meet Gran Torino**, and they learn from All For One's imprisoned associate that One For All has had **eight previous wielders**, and that Midoriya may be able to commune with them.

**Results:** All of Class 1-A except **Bakugo** and **Todoroki** pass. Both failed for interpersonal reasons — Bakugo for insulting an examiner, Todoroki for his cold demeanour. They must attend a **remedial course**.

**Characters introduced:** Inasa Yoarashi, Yo Shindo, Seiji Shishikura, Utsushimi Camie, Mei Hatsume (expanded), Gang Orca, Ms. Joke, Hound Dog.

---

### 4. Shie Hassaikai Arc (opening) — Episodes 62–63

The season's final two episodes introduce the next arc: Midoriya encounters a young girl named **Eri** and a yakuza boss named **Overhaul** in the street. Episode 63, "Unrivaled," establishes Sir Nighteye's agency and the beginning of the hero work-study placements.

## Themes

### The Failure of Institutions
Every institution in Season 3 fails. U.A. fails to protect Bakugo. The government fails to act quickly enough. The hero ranking system fails to provide any real safety — because safety was never the product of institutions, only of one man. When he is gone, the whole structure is revealed to have been hollow.

### Symbols
All Might's retirement is explicitly framed as the loss of a **symbol**, not a soldier. The series argues that symbols matter — that people behave better when they believe someone is watching — and that a society built entirely on one symbol is a society with no foundation. Endeavor's arc in Season 4 is the direct response: an attempt to build a symbol out of someone who never wanted to be one.

### Reconciliation
The Deku vs. Kacchan Part 2 fight is one of the least flattering portrayals of its protagonist in the series. Midoriya is shown as dishonest for hiding the truth; Bakugo as emotionally incapable of processing it. Their reconciliation is not a friendship — it is a mutual agreement to stop lying to each other.

## Production Notes

- Opening themes: "**Odd Future**" by UVERworld and "**Make My Story**" by Lenny code fiction.
- Ending themes: "**Update**" by miwa and "**Long Hope Philia**" by Masaki Suda (written by Takahiro Moriuchi of ONE OK ROCK).
- Episodes 47–49, covering the Kamino Ward battle, are among the most highly rated in the entire anime and are frequently cited as the series' animation peak.
