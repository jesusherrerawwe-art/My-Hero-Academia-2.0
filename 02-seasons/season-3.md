Season 3 is the series' pivot. Everything before it is a school story with escalating stakes; everything after it takes place in a world that has lost its anchor. The Kamino Ward battle in episode 48 is the single most consequential episode of the entire franchise.

---

## Synopsis

Class 1-A travels to a mountain training facility run by the hero team **Wild, Wild Pussycats** for a summer training camp; the five students who failed their practical finals attend alongside everyone else, because Aizawa judges that they need the training most. The camp is attacked on its first night by the League of Villains' **Vanguard Action Squad**, a team of over ten villains whose primary objective is to capture Katsuki Bakugo alive.

Bakugo is taken. Five of his classmates — Midoriya, Iida, Todoroki, Kirishima and Yaoyorozu — go after him against direct orders, and the attempt forces **All Might** into the open at Kamino Ward, where he finally faces **All For One** in person.

All Might wins. It costs him everything: his remaining power, his career, and his secret. In front of a live national broadcast, the Symbol of Peace deflates into a gaunt, coughing old man and points at the camera. Villainy stops being a fringe criminal enterprise and becomes a mass movement. All For One is returned to prison, but his ideology is loose.

In the aftermath, U.A. moves Class 1-A into dorms, and the students sit the **Provisional Hero Licence Exam** — the qualification that legally permits a student to act as a hero. Bakugo and Todoroki fail on conduct grounds and are assigned a remedial course. The season ends with the class beginning professional work-studies, and Midoriya encountering a girl named **Eri** for the first time.

## Arc-by-Arc Breakdown

### 1. Forest Training Camp Arc — Episodes 39–45 (chapters 70–83)

**What happens:** The five students who failed their final exams — Sato, Kirishima, Ashido, Kaminari and Sero — attend a remedial course while the rest of the class trains. The camp is run by the four-member hero team **Wild, Wild Pussycats**: Mandalay, Pixie-Bob, Ragdoll and Tiger.

> **Episode 39, "Game Start,"** is largely a recap of Season 2 with original framing scenes. It is one of the series' five filler episodes.

**Ragdoll's Search Quirk** allows the team to locate and assess every student's abilities instantly, which is how the camp is tailored to each individual: Bakugo is pushed to create larger explosions until his arms give out, Todoroki alternates fire and ice until he loses feeling in both halves, Midoriya works on Full Cowl percentages.

**The attack.** On the first night the Vanguard Action Squad arrives — nine villains sent specifically to capture Bakugo and, secondarily, to collect Quirk material:

| Villain | Quirk | Role in the attack |
|---|---|---|
| **Dabi** | Blueflame (Cremation) | Clashes twice with Aizawa, who erases his flames; deliberately taunts the heroes |
| **Himiko Toga** | Transform | Ambush-squad close fighter; later targets Uraraka |
| **Muscular** | Muscle Augmentation | Attacks Kota; nearly kills Midoriya |
| **Moonfish** | Blade-Tooth | Hunts students through the forest; slices off one of Shoji's tentacles and is ultimately crushed by the berserk Dark Shadow |
| **Mustard** | Gas (unnamed) | Floods the training grounds with a poisonous, sleep-inducing gas; fights Kendo and Tetsutetsu |
| **Mr. Compress** | Compress | Captures Bakugo (and, briefly, Tokoyami) by turning them into marbles |
| **Magne** | Magnetism | Close-range fighter; clashes with Tiger |
| **Spinner** | Gecko-like physiology | Ideologue; carries a sword made of knives |
| **Twice** | Double | Creates clones; mentally unstable |
| **Kurogiri** | Warp Gate | Transport and coordination (captured much later) |

**Aizawa's stand.** Shota Aizawa moves through the forest efficiently neutralising dozens of the League's low-tier villains, and twice confronts Dabi, erasing his fire (the first "Dabi" he erases is a Twice clone). He takes some cuts and bruises but is *not* seriously maimed this arc, and is on his feet directing the clean-up afterward.

> **Correction to a widely repeated claim:** Aizawa does **not** lose his eye or his leg in this arc, and he is not hospitalised for weeks here. His severe U.S.J. injuries happened back in Season 1. The loss of his right eye and the amputation of his right leg happen much later, during the **Paranormal Liberation War** (Season 6).

**Ragdoll is targeted specifically.** All For One wants her Search Quirk, and the squad's orders include capturing her. She is found alive in the woods after the attack, but Search has been permanently torn out of her by All For One — she is left Quirkless — the first on-screen proof that a Quirk can be taken from a living holder.

**Midoriya vs. Muscular (ep. 42).** Midoriya finds Kota Izumi — a boy who hates heroes because his parents, both Pro Heroes, died saving strangers — cornered by Muscular. Midoriya puts himself between them and takes Muscular's full force, then pushes One For All past every percentage he has ever used: **1,000,000%**. He wins. Both of his arms are destroyed.

Kota's arc is the season's moral argument in miniature: hero society consumes the children of heroes, and the series refuses to pretend otherwise.

**Bakugo is taken (ep. 45).** Bakugo and Todoroki fight their way through the compound. Mr. Compress catches Bakugo off-guard and compresses him into a marble, escaping with Kurogiri. The season's second half is now a rescue story.

### 2. Hideout Raid Arc / Kamino Ward — Episodes 46–49 (chapters 84–97)

**What happens:** The police and Pro Heroes locate the League's hideout in Kamino Ward. Midoriya, Iida, Todoroki, Kirishima and Yaoyorozu go in ahead of them, in disguise, without permission.

**The rescue.** They find Bakugo, who is furious at being rescued and refuses to leave quietly — he does not want to owe Midoriya anything. As they escape, **Tomura Shigaraki** and **All For One** intercept them. All For One intends to take Bakugo and corrupt him; Shigaraki intends to kill everyone present, starting with Midoriya.

**All Might arrives.** He is still technically active, but his injury limits his muscle form to mere seconds of fighting time. He comes anyway, knowing this may be his last battle.

#### All Might vs. All For One (eps. 47–49)

The fight is structured as an argument as much as a battle:

- **All For One reveals himself** as the man who killed **Nana Shimura**, All Might's mentor and the seventh holder of One For All — a fact he had kept hidden for decades.
- All For One's Quirk allows him to **steal Quirks from others and give them away**; the Nomu, Kurogiri's Warp Gate and Shigaraki's Decay were all manufactured from stolen power. He is not one villain but an accumulated arsenal.
- All Might is running on the last embers of One For All — enough for perhaps a handful of strikes.
- He wins with **United States of Smash**, a punch so large it changes the weather.
- All For One is defeated but survives, and is returned to Tartarus.

**The cost.** All Might's muscle form fails in front of a live national broadcast. The entire country watches the Symbol of Peace collapse into Toshinori Yagi — gaunt, coughing blood, unable to stand. He points at the camera and says:

> *"Next… it's your turn."*

It is intended as reassurance. It is received as an announcement of abandonment.

### 3. Aftermath — Episode 50 (chapters 97–98)

**The consequences are immediate and structural:**

- All Might **retires** permanently. He becomes a U.A. faculty member, teaching a hero basics class.
- The media blames U.A. and specifically **Bakugo** for All Might's retirement — the narrative being that the League targeted him and the Symbol of Peace died rescuing him.
- Public confidence in hero institutions collapses. Villain sympathisers, emboldened, begin to organise openly.
- U.A. moves Class 1-A into on-campus dorms (**Heights Alliance**) so that students can be protected and supervised.
- Aizawa tells the class directly that they are now being trained to fight, not to perform.

Episode 50 is titled *"End of the Beginning, Beginning of the End"* — and it means it. Everything after this point is a countdown.

### 4. Provisional Hero Licence Exam Arc — Episodes 51–62 (chapters 98–121)

**What happens:** To legally act as heroes in emergencies, students must hold a **Provisional Hero Licence**. The exam is administered by the **Hero Public Safety Commission** and overseen by examiner **Yokumiru Mera**, a visibly, permanently overworked functionary who survives on cigarettes and sleep deprivation.

Students from across Japan compete. The two rival schools given the most screen time:

| School | Notable students |
|---|---|
| **Shiketsu High** | **Inasa Yoarashi** (Gale) · **Yo Shindo** (Vibrate) · **Seiji Shishikura** (Meatball) · **Camie Utsushimi** (Glamour) |
| **Ketsubutsu Academy** | **Tatami Nakagame** (Turtle Neck; Telescopic) · **Shikkui Kaori** · **Nagamasa Mora** |

Also supervising the exam is the Pro Hero **Ms. Joke** (Emi Fukukado, Quirk Outburst), Aizawa's loud, joke-telling professional rival, who has repeatedly teased him about marriage.

**Inasa Yoarashi** is the season's most important new character. He is as passionate and loud as All Might, and he **refused Endeavor's recommendation** to U.A. — he hates Endeavor for the look in his eyes. He is a mirror of Todoroki's problem, approached from the opposite direction.

#### First Exam — Rescue

Examinees are dropped into a simulated city collapsing under disaster and must rescue civilians while cooperating with strangers. Pro Heroes **Hound Dog** and **Gang Orca** serve among the examiners.

The exam's actual test is not rescue skill — it is **whether you can work with people you do not know, under pressure, without letting personal feeling interfere**. Midoriya, Iida, Uraraka, Asui, Kirishima and most of Class 1-A pass.

**Two students fail on conduct:**
- **Bakugo** — for harsh, uncooperative conduct during the rescue phase (his immediate strike on the double-crossing Yo Shindo is cited as part of the pattern), and for acting entirely alone.
- **Todoroki** — for refusing to cooperate and for the cold hostility Inasa identifies as the same look he despises in Endeavor.

Neither failure is about ability. Both are about temperament, which is precisely the point.

#### Second Exam — Gang Orca

The second round pits examinees against **Gang Orca**, then the **No. 10 Pro Hero** (the ranking drops to No. 12 after the chart update in Season 4), who deliberately plays a terrifying invading villain. Most examinees freeze; those who pass hold their nerve and keep protecting civilians while fighting him off.

**The result:** Bakugo and Todoroki do not receive licences. Both are assigned a **remedial course** under Gang Orca himself — which becomes the Remedial Course arc early in Season 4.

#### Deku vs. Kacchan, Part 2 (eps. 59–61)

After the exam, Bakugo corners Midoriya at night at **Ground Beta** and forces a fight. He has spent months unable to reconcile what he knows: All Might retired the same day Midoriya's power began to change, and Midoriya has been getting stronger in a way that looks like **inheritance**.

Aizawa catches them. All Might arrives and tells Bakugo the whole truth — One For All, All For One, Nana Shimura, the embers, all of it.

Bakugo's reaction is not triumph. He breaks down. He understands that All Might's retirement is his fault, that Midoriya was chosen over him, and that the person he spent ten years tormenting was the one carrying the weight. It is the first time in the series Bakugo is genuinely humbled.

#### The Big Three (ep. 62)

U.A.'s third-years — **Mirio Togata**, **Tamaki Amajiki** and **Nejire Hado** — are introduced as the strongest students in the school, and as the standard Class 1-A is being measured against. Mirio, in particular, is positioned as the candidate All Might *should* have chosen.

### 5. Shie Hassaikai Arc Begins — Episode 63 (chapter 122+)

Episode 63 opens the Overhaul storyline that occupies most of Season 4. Class 1-A begins professional work-studies, and Midoriya encounters a small girl named **Eri** for the first time — accompanied by **Kai Chisaki**, who calls himself **Overhaul**.

## Themes

### Institutions Cannot Protect You
The training camp attack succeeds partly because U.A. told the students exactly where they would be. The rescue mission succeeds partly because five students broke the rules. The series is explicit that the systems meant to protect children failed, and that the adults' response is to build a wall — dorms — rather than to fix the problem.

### The Symbol Was Always Fragile
All Might's defeat is not a loss of strength but a loss of **performance**. He had been bluffing for years. The moment the bluff ends, society's confidence goes with it, and the season spends its second half watching ordinary people decide what heroism means without a figurehead.

### Inherited Guilt
Three characters carry guilt they did not earn this season: Bakugo, blamed publicly for All Might's retirement; Todoroki, hated by Inasa for his father's reputation; and Kota, orphaned by parents who died doing their jobs. The season argues that hero society distributes its costs unevenly and mostly onto children.

### Passion Without Direction
Inasa Yoarashi is the season's most interesting new character precisely because he is *right* about Endeavor and *wrong* in how he acts on it. His arc — being made to fight alongside Todoroki, the son of the man he despises — is the licence exam's real lesson.

## Production Notes

- **Opening themes:**
  - "ODD FUTURE" — **UVERworld** (eps. 39–50)
  - "Make my story" — **Lenny code fiction** (eps. 51–63)
- **Ending themes:**
  - "Update" — **miwa** (eps. 39–50)
  - "Long Hope Philia" — **Masaki Suda** (eps. 51–63)
- "Long Hope Philia" doubles as the theme song for the film *My Hero Academia: Two Heroes*, which is why Masaki Suda voices a character in that film.
- ***My Hero Academia: Two Heroes*** was released in Japan on **August 3, 2018** — mid-season, between episodes 57 and 58. Episode 58 exists specifically to set it up. The film is set before the Kamino battle and features All Might at the height of his power.
- Episode 48, *"Symbol of Peace,"* is the most-watched and most-discussed episode of the series. Its final sequence — All Might's muscle form failing on live television — is frequently cited as one of the greatest moments in modern shonen anime.
- The Kamino fight was animated with an unusually large team; the "United States of Smash" sequence became the series' visual signature.
- Season 3 was the first to be simulcast widely outside Japan and the point at which the series broke into mainstream Western anime conversation.
