## The Narrative Shape of Seasons 1–5

Across these 113 episodes the series follows a clear escalation pattern:

1. **Season 1 — A boy who has nothing.** Midoriya begins powerless and ends the season having nearly died twice. The stakes are personal and small-scale: one school, one facility, one villain.

2. **Season 2 — A boy who has to prove he deserves it.** The Sports Festival is a public audition; the Stain arc introduces the idea that hero society itself may be corrupt. The final exams end with Midoriya's secret nearly exposed.

3. **Season 3 — The world loses its anchor.** The Kamino Ward battle is the series' turning point. All Might defeats All For One, but in doing so he reveals his weakness to the entire world and retires. Villainy stops being an aberration and becomes a movement.

4. **Season 4 — The cost of heroism.** The Shie Hassaikai arc shows what heroes fail to prevent: Mirio Togata loses his Quirk permanently, Sir Nighteye dies, and Eri is rescued only after years of abuse. The arc is the most pessimistic in the first five seasons — and the Pro Hero arc responds by showing Endeavor trying, painfully, to become a symbol worth believing in.

5. **Season 5 — The villains get a story.** For the first time, a full arc is told almost entirely from the villains' perspective. The Meta Liberation Army arc explains where Shigaraki came from and reveals the identity of Dabi. The season ends with both sides fully mobilised for total war.
