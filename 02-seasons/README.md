## ⚠️ Season 5 Reordered the Manga's Arcs

This is the single most confusing thing about Season 5, and worth flagging explicitly.

**In the manga**, the order is: Joint Training (ch. 194–217) → **Meta Liberation Army** (ch. 218–240) → **Endeavor Agency** (ch. 241–252) → Paranormal Liberation War (ch. 253+).

**In the anime**, the Endeavor Agency arc was moved *earlier*, so the broadcast order became: Joint Training → **Endeavor Agency** → **Meta Liberation Army** → Paranormal Liberation War.

The reordering is a deliberate structural choice, and it works: it lets the season end on **Shigaraki's origin** and the formation of the Paranormal Liberation Front — the biggest cliffhanger available — rather than on Endeavor's family drama. It also means manga readers and anime-only viewers experienced Season 5 in genuinely different orders, which caused considerable confusion at the time.

Episode 107, *"More of a Hero Than Anyone,"* belongs to the Paranormal Liberation War arc (adapting ch. 253–254) even though it aired between the Endeavor Agency and Meta Liberation Army episodes.

## The Narrative Shape of Seasons 1–5

Across these 113 episodes the series follows a clear escalation:

1. **Season 1 — A boy who has nothing.** Midoriya begins powerless and ends the season having nearly died twice. The stakes are small: one school, one facility, one villain.

2. **Season 2 — A boy who has to prove he deserves it.** The Sports Festival is a public audition; the Stain arc introduces the idea that hero society itself may be corrupt. The final exams end with Midoriya's secret nearly exposed.

3. **Season 3 — The world loses its anchor.** The Kamino Ward battle is the series' turning point. All Might defeats All For One but reveals his weakness to the entire country and retires. Villainy stops being an aberration and becomes a movement.

4. **Season 4 — The cost of heroism.** The Shie Hassaikai arc shows what heroes fail to prevent: Mirio Togata loses his Quirk permanently, Sir Nighteye dies, and Eri is rescued only after years of abuse. The Pro Hero arc responds by showing Endeavor trying, painfully, to become a symbol worth believing in.

5. **Season 5 — The villains get a story.** For the first time, two full arcs are told almost entirely from the villains' perspective. It explains exactly where Shigaraki came from, shows the League acquiring a political movement with over a hundred thousand members, and leaves Dabi's hatred of Endeavor as an unanswered question hanging over the Todoroki family. The season ends with both sides fully mobilised for total war.

## Filler and Special Episodes

The series is remarkably filler-free. Different guides list either five or six non-canon episodes across all 113 episodes of Seasons 1–5:

| Episode | Title | Type |
|---|---|---|
| 13.5 | Hero Notebook | Season 1 recap special |
| 32 | Everyone's Internships | Covers the other students' work-studies |
| 39 | Game Start | Season 2 recap with original framing |
| 58 | Save the World with Love! | Lead-in to the film *Two Heroes* |
| 64 | The Scoop on U.A. Class 1-A | Anime-original; introduces journalist Taneo Tokuda |
| 104 | Long Time No See, Selkie | Lead-in to the film *World Heroes' Mission* |

Most guides count **five** filler episodes — 13.5, 39, 58, 64 and 104 — treating episode 32 as canon-adjacent. All are skippable without losing plot, though 58 and 104 are worth watching if you intend to see the corresponding films.
