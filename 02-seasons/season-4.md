# Season 4 (2019–2020)

*"I will save you."*

---

Season 4 is the series' most emotionally punishing. Where earlier seasons asked whether Midoriya was worthy of his power, Season 4 asks what heroism costs the people who fail — and it gives an unflinching answer.

---

## Arc-by-Arc Breakdown

### 1. Shie Hassaikai Arc — Episodes 64–77

**What happens:** U.A.'s second semester begins, and first-years apply for **hero work-study** placements with Pro Hero agencies. Midoriya, along with Uraraka, Asui, and others, is taken on by **Sir Nighteye** — All Might's former sidekick, whose Quirk **Foresight** lets him see one hour of a person's future once per day.

Nighteye's agency is investigating the **Shie Hassaikai**, a yakuza group led by **Kai Chisaki**, known as **Overhaul**. Chisaki's Quirk lets him disassemble and reassemble anything he touches — including human bodies. He is accompanied by a small girl named **Eri**, who is visibly terrified of him and whom he claims is his daughter.

**The truth about Eri:**
- Eri's Quirk is **Rewind** — she can reverse a person's body to a previous state.
- Overhaul has been **murdering her repeatedly** and rewinding her body in order to harvest her cells.
- From her blood he manufactures **Quirk-Destroying Bullets**, which erase a person's Quirk permanently when fired.
- She has been held captive, abused, and told that her existence is a curse.

**Key moments:**

**Mirio Togata — "Lemillion."** The most important character introduction of the season. Mirio is the U.A. third-year whom Nighteye groomed to inherit One For All before All Might chose Midoriya. His Quirk, **Permeation**, lets him phase through anything — including the ground, air, and light — making it extraordinarily difficult to control. He is cheerful, physically overwhelming, and completely dedicated.

During the raid, Mirio fights Overhaul alone to buy Eri time to escape. He is shot with a Quirk-Destroying Bullet. **He loses his Quirk permanently** — and still continues fighting, unarmed, until backup arrives. It is one of the most devastating sequences in the series: a boy who spent years mastering an unmasterable power loses it in a single second, and keeps going anyway.

**The raid.** A joint operation involving Nighteye's agency, the police, Fat Gum, Ryukyu, Bubble Girl, Centipeder, Rock Lock, and others infiltrates the Hassaikai's underground labyrinth. Students including Kirishima, Fat Gum's charge **Tamaki Amajiki**, and Ryukyu's interns fight the yakuza's elite — Chronostasis, Mimic, Rappa, and the masked Kendo.

**Midoriya vs. Overhaul.** Midoriya fights at 100% throughout, sustaining catastrophic damage. Overhaul fuses with a subordinate to become a monstrous form. Midoriya is nearly killed — until **Eri, screaming, activates Rewind on him**, reversing his body to a state before his injuries. He defeats Overhaul with a single 1,000,000% Delaware Detroit Smash.

Eri's Rewind is so powerful that she nearly rewinds Midoriya out of existence. Mirio carries her out.

**Sir Nighteye dies.** Foresight had shown him Midoriya's death, and he had spent the entire arc trying to change that future. He is mortally wounded by Overhaul during the raid. As he dies, he reconciles with All Might — the two had not spoken in years over a disagreement about Midoriya — and tells him he should have smiled more. His final words are that he wants Midoriya to have a **bright future**.

**Consequences:**
- Overhaul's arms are destroyed by Shigaraki in a post-credits confrontation; the League absorbs the remnants of the yakuza.
- Eri is placed in a hospital and begins, slowly, to recover. She smiles for the first time.
- Midoriya's arms are healed by Rewind, but the event proves his body is not built for One For All.
- Mirio is left Quirkless. The class's brightest star becomes its most poignant casualty.

### 2. Remedial Course Arc — Episodes 78–80

**What happens:** Bakugo and Todoroki attend the remedial training required to re-sit the Provisional Licence Exam, supervised by **Gang Orca**.

The arc is short but structurally important: it puts the two most powerful students in Class 1-A in a position of humility, working under a Pro Hero who treats them as incompetent children. Gang Orca's critique of both — Bakugo's attitude and Todoroki's emotional distance — is the series' clearest statement that raw power is not the same as being a hero.

Episode 78, "Smoldering Flames," also revisits Endeavor's fight against the Nomu from Season 3, framing his struggle to become a legitimate symbol.

---

### 3. U.A. School Festival Arc — Episodes 81–86

**What happens:** After the trauma of the Shie Hassaikai raid, Class 1-A decides to perform at the U.A. School Festival — a dance-and-music show intended to remind people that the school is still a place of joy.

Simultaneously, a small-time villain named **Gentle Criminal** — a man who failed the U.A. entrance exam twice, was expelled from hero society, and became a criminal for attention — plans to break into the festival to make a name for himself. His partner, **La Brava**, whose Quirk lets her grant a one-time power boost to someone she loves, supports him.

**Key moments:**
- Class 1-A rehearses: Kyoka Jiro and Momo Yaoyorozu lead the music; Mina Ashido choreographs the dance.
- Midoriya encounters Gentle in the forest near U.A. and defeats him — but Gentle's monologue about what it means to be discarded by hero society lands.
- **La Brava's love powers Gentle's Elasticity to an enormous degree**, and he nearly overwhelms Midoriya.
- Midoriya wins not through force but by understanding: he tells Gentle that his dream was never really villainy. Gentle surrenders, and La Brava is arrested with him.
- The festival performance goes ahead. It is bright, warm, and deliberately anticlimactic — a full episode of students dancing and playing music after a season of child abuse and death.

**Why this arc matters:** It is the series' explicit statement about what heroism is *for*. Not victory, but the ordinary happiness of people who feel safe. Horikoshi places it immediately after the darkest arc he had written.

### 4. Pro Hero Arc — Episodes 87–88

**What happens:** The **Japanese Hero Billboard Chart** ceremony. With All Might retired, the rankings are restructured:

- **Endeavor becomes the No. 1 Hero.**
- **Hawks becomes No. 2.**
- **Best Jeanist** is presumed dead (actually in a coma).

The ceremony is not a celebration. Endeavor's promotion is greeted with a mixture of applause and open hostility, given his history of abusing his family and his obsession with surpassing All Might.

Endeavor takes the stage and, rather than boast, says only that he will **make amends** — that he will try to become a hero worthy of the position. He asks the public to watch him and judge him.

**Hawks.** The No. 2 Hero approaches Endeavor afterward and invites him to dinner. Hawks is fast, flippant, and apparently carefree — but he is, in fact, working **undercover inside the Paranormal Liberation Front**, having infiltrated the League of Villains under the Hero Public Safety Commission's orders. He tells Endeavor that a war is coming, and that the heroes need to prepare.

Episode 88, "His Start," closes the season with Endeavor beginning his slow, painful, and largely unrewarded attempt at redemption — and with the audience knowing that Hawks is in far more danger than he admits.

**Characters introduced:** Kai Chisaki / Overhaul, Eri, Sir Nighteye, Chronostasis, Mimic, Kendo Rappa, Gentle Criminal, La Brava, Hawks (expanded), Bubble Girl, Centipeder, Fat Gum (expanded), Ryukyu.

## Themes

### The Cost of Being Too Late
The Shie Hassaikai arc is built around failure. Eri was abused for years while the heroes investigated. Nighteye saw Midoriya's death coming and could not prevent it — only redirect it. Mirio lost everything because he chose to do the right thing one second too slowly. The heroes win the raid and lose almost everything that mattered.

### Redemption Is Not Forgiveness
Endeavor's arc is the series' most careful moral argument. He does not apologise adequately. He does not receive forgiveness from his family. His wife will not look at him; his son will barely speak to him. What he receives instead is an *obligation* — the No. 1 rank, and the requirement to earn it. Horikoshi refuses to resolve this, and that refusal is the point.

### What Heroes Are For
The School Festival arc answers the season's despair directly. Gentleness, music, and a school dance are presented as legitimate heroic outcomes — not filler, but the thing being protected.
