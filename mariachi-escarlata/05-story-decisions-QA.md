# PART I — THE READING EXPERIENCE

### Q1. What is the prologue?
- **A) ⭐ Guadalajara, age 6 — the two weeks of itching and the morning the skin sheds.** A normal kitchen, a terrified boy, parents who do not scream. Ends on his mother's first handmade mask or his first pan dulce after.
- B) Cold open in Sinaloa at 14 — the ambush, the hooves on stone, the pact — then rewind to Japan.
- C) The San Juan de Dios market rescue at 10 — the public origin of "El Niño Calavera."
- D) In media res — his first night in the Heights Alliance dorm, with Guadalajara unfolding as flashbacks across Volume I.

**⭐ Justification for A:** it starts the book on the novel's thesis question (*what does a loving world do with a monstrous face?*) and immediately plants the Tenko parallel (the itch) as dramatic irony readers only cash in at the finale. The Sinaloa pact is your best MYSTERY — spending it in the prologue wastes the reveal; it lands harder as the late-Volume-I cantina flashback after readers already love him. C is a great chapter 2 or Entreacto, not an opener. D front-loads confusion before we care.

### Q2. Narrative point of view
- **A) ⭐ Third-person limited, locked tightly on Chuy for the whole book** (we only know what he knows; other viewpoints exist solely as Entreacto documents: notebook pages, headlines, transcripts).
- B) First-person Chuy ("I policed my heartbeat before I turned the corner…") — classic light-novel/Wattpad voice.
- C) Rotating third-person (Chuy, Deku, Jirō, the Charro each get POV chapters).
- D) Third-person Chuy + first-person cantina interludes in the Charro's voice.

**⭐ Justification for A:** third limited gives you intimate access AND the ensemble war scenes the finale needs, while the Entreacto documents (Ana Sofía's case file, Televisa clippings, dream transcripts) smuggle in other voices without scattering the spotlight — the book must always feel like *Chuy's* story. B is the legitimate Wattpad-native alternative and I'll happily write it if you prefer that closeness; my only caution is that first-person makes the war's multi-front finale and the Mictlán descent much harder to stage. C risks exactly what you warned against — the book becoming an ensemble recap with your OC in it.

### Q3. How does Spanish live on the page?
- **A) ⭐ Natural code-switching:** Spanish phrases, prayers, food and nicknames appear unapologetically, but every phrase is made understandable through context the first time; no brackets, no footnotes. The Charro always speaks old, formal Spanish rendered in italics with meaning carried by scene. A short glossary sits at volume's end for fun.
- B) English-only except signature phrases (heroe, mijo, vámonos).
- C) Bold untranslated Spanish everywhere with a running glossary — maximal authenticity, maximal reader friction.
- D) Every Spanish line followed by an inline translation.

**⭐ Justification for A:** it reads like how bicultural families actually talk, it's the standard in successful crossover fiction (*Coco*, *Blue Beetle* adjacent prose), and it treats your Houston audience — heavily bilingual — with respect while never stranding an English-only Wattpad reader. D kills rhythm and feels like a textbook.

### Q4. Tone and genre mix
- **A) ⭐ Warm school-life heart with rising mythic dread — roughly 45% charm/relationships, 30% action, 15% horror/afterlife, 10% comedy-peak.** MHA's own ratio, mariachi-colored.
- B) Dark war-and-horror novel with comic relief only.
- C) Cozy slice-of-life with occasional villain arcs.
- D) Full tragic opera from chapter 1.

**⭐ Justification for A:** your premise is a horror engine (soul quotas, possession, corpse faces) bolted to a candy-store family and a boy who sings lullabies inside shield domes. The horror only works if readers first feel safe in Guadalajara and the dorm. The ratio lets the Mictlán act play as earned darkness rather than a different book.

### Q5. How much canon do we actually retell? [LOCKED-DIRECTION — confirm]
- **A) ⭐ Chuy-scene-only canon:** no canon event gets a scene unless Chuy makes a decision inside it; otherwise it's one paragraph, a news clip, a hallway rumor. Three-chapter cap per canon arc. ~60% original / 40% refracted canon.
- B) Full retellings of canon fights with Chuy inserted alongside.
- C) Alternating chapters: one canon chapter, one Chuy chapter.
- D) Canon exists only as background news; Chuy never participates in canon events.

**⭐ Justification for A:** you said it yourself — the manga is free; nobody needs our recap. D is too detached (the license exam and war ARE his story). A keeps maximum canon texture with zero filler.

# PART II — THE BIOLOGY & THE CURSE

### Q6. Was the age-6 skull face the Charro's pre-selection mark?
- **A) ⭐ Ambiguous until ~90% through; final answer YES** — the mutation was the contract beginning to write itself on him before any signature; Chuy's climax act is reclaiming the face (*"Mi cara no es tuya. Yo elijo qué significa."*)
- B) Pure coincidence — an ordinary mutant Quirk; the Charro only found him at 14.
- C) Explicitly supernatural from chapter one (doctors in Mexico always suspected).
- D) It's an inherited family Quirk unrelated to the entity.

**⭐ Justification for A:** the delayed manifestation (6y3 vs the norm of ~4) is already a waving flag; answering YES at the end converts his oldest wound (the face) into the final battleground of free will — the exact thing 「俺は、俺のヒーローになる」promises. Keeping it ambiguous until then gives you dozens of dream-debates where the Charro *claims* the face and Chuy can't yet prove otherwise. B wastes your best planted mystery; C resolves it too early and makes him chosen-by-destiny instead of self-made.

### Q7. How much of his skeleton is reinforced?
- **A) ⭐ Skull only** (frontal plate 800 PSI / 15 cm concrete), weaker jaw and back of head, 5–6 hit bell-ring limit, and crucially **the neck and brain still absorb every impact** — concussions are real.
- B) Full skeleton at 3.4× density.
- C) Skull now; the cape spreads ossification over the series.
- D) Full skeleton, but the weight slows him (heavier muscle cost).

**⭐ Justification for A:** a one-piece armor plate with a glass jaw(ish) is writable, drawable, and choreographable — every fight has a protected angle and a panic button. Full skeleton erases the underdog math you worked hard to build and invites "why doesn't he just headbutt Shigaraki forever."

### Q8. What gates and costs the cape? (select the rule stack)
- **A) ⭐ Full stack:** (1) heart rate >110 triggers it involuntarily; (2) it is JUDGMENT-LOCKED — inert cold wind on non-corrupt targets, unusable against classmates; (3) the Charro can withhold cooperation mid-fight as leverage; (4) each heavy use advances the fissures ~1 mm and causes soul-fatigue Recovery Girl can't heal; (5) it also moves autonomously to save people he didn't see.
- B) Fully voluntary, full control, standard stamina cost.
- C) Sentient with its own dominant agenda — Chuy is mostly along for the ride.
- D) Nighttime-only power (Dark Shadow style).

**⭐ Justification for A:** each rule protects a different story: (2) is the training-underdog engine; (3) gives the dream-debates teeth (he has to *negotiate* with his Quirk); (4) makes friendships and rest narratively load-bearing (Part 3 proved validation literally paused the spread); (5) sets up the mandatory horror scene where it tries to claim without him. Note folded rulings: Aizawa's Erasure makes the FLAMES flicker and fail (his first proof it's not a Quirk) while the physical cape and skull persist; Eri's Rewind can push back fissure tissue but never the soul-contract.

### Q9. Is there a Mercy Override?
- **A) ⭐ Yes:** Chuy can physically shield a claimable black-stained soul from the cape — at a personal price (a surge of fissure spread, or "interest" on his debt, or one favor owed to the Charro). One recurring claimable villain becomes his personal redemption project (his Gentle/Nagant figure).
- B) No override — the flame verdict is absolute; his drama is fighting to arrest before the cape collects.
- C) Override always works, free, no cost.
- D) Override exists but can ONLY be activated by playing his trumpet (mercy as music).

**⭐ Justification for A:** without it, "I believe in second chances" is a belief he never gets to ACT on — the book would be about a boy who disagrees with his Quirk but obeys it. The cost keeps every mercy agonizing and quota-relevant. D is gorgeous flavor — I'd suggest folding it INTO A: the override requires his hands AND the song, willpower with a soundtrack.

### Q10. What does soul-sight actually see?
- **A) ⭐ Three-tier stain taxonomy:** *limpio* (nothing), **gris** (ego, vice, harm-without-profit — Bakugo's opening reading; Endeavor), **negro** (almas podridas, claimable). Greys visibly FADE as people atone; darken as they fall.
- B) Binary: stain or no stain.
- C) A full color spectrum labeling every category of sin.
- D) Chuy sees nothing; the cape decides silently and he only learns verdicts after the fact.

**⭐ Justification for A:** grey is where 95% of real people live, and fading greys are a visual PROOF that souls change — which is the empirical evidence the Charro's entire false premise ("no redemption") can't survive. Bakugo's grey clearing over 2–3 weeks is the first data point; Gentle's grey evaporating after the festival is the second; the tribunal in Act 3 gets a photo album of fading stains.

### Q11. What exactly does bone-conduction hearing detect?
- **A) ⭐ STRESS, not lies:** heart-rate spikes and tendon vibration. A nervous truth, a crush, a terrified child read the same as deception — he must interpret. Crowds/noise blank it; concentration and quiet required.
- B) Reliable lie detection.
- C) Literal intent (he hears what someone plans to do).
- D) Combat-only motion tracking.

**⭐ Justification for A:** a working lie detector nukes half the canon plot (All Might's secret, Aoyama the traitor, Camie/Toga). Stress-detection PRESERVES those mysteries while enabling the Aoyama beat you want: Chuy hears his heart hammering for months and assumes he's just weirdly anxious. It also produces romance scenes no other couple can have (he knows his own pulse spikes around Jirō and cannot lie to himself about it).

### Q12. What can skull-only Chuy DO offensively?
- **A) ⭐ Full package:** (1) predictive close-quarters grappling/sweeps read from floor vibrations (an unflashy, lucha-grounded style Mirko later sharpens); (2) Hatsume-built **sonic trumpet support gear** (directional grito blast / concussive pulse / shield-shatter); (3) his costume spurs as cape-anchor points and combat tempo metronomes; (4) glide requires a surface/shadow tether — never true flight.
- B) Nothing — pure defense; he loses every spar forever.
- C) The trumpet stays music-only; give him bone-shaping offensive techniques instead.
- D) He wins spars through intimidation spam alone.

**⭐ Justification for A:** losing 7/10 forever makes him a joke, not an underdog; he needs a path to hard-fought skull-only wins through SKILL (earned, not firepower). The sonic trumpet merges combat and the music thesis and builds the Act 3 instrument that follows him into Mictlán. B is too humiliating for a 16-year-old veteran of cartel raids; C drifts toward generic bone-Quirk territory and away from his themes.

### Q13. What happens if the fissures reach his heart?
- **A) ⭐ 100% succession:** the Charro completes the replacement — Chuy becomes the next collector, body alive, boy gone. Ana Sofía logs dated millimeter measurements beside the soul count as the book's second countdown. Love, music, sleep and community measurably SLOW the spread; isolation accelerates it.
- B) Physical death.
- C) Never defined — vague doom.
- D) Eri can fully rewind it away, no stakes.

**⭐ Justification for A:** it makes relationships a combat mechanic (every friendship is armor), gives the Dark Deku-style isolation chapter real danger (if Chuy goes lone vigilante, the cracks sprint), and offers the most horrifying stakes in the genre: not death, but *becoming him*. C is lazy; D deletes the tension; B is smaller than the legend deserves.

# PART III — THE HORSE & THE VOICE

### Q14. Name, timing, and rules of the horse?
- **A) ⭐ (recommended bundle):** it is **unnamed in-text** ("el Caballo del Charro" — property of the entity) until the Act 3 descent, where Chuy mounts WITHOUT FEAR (paying off dad's lesson), earns it, and names it **Tizón** (ember/brand). Summoning rule: it answers only when he asks for **someone else's sake**, never to save himself. The Charro CAN repossess it mid-ride (ticking clock). Koda becomes its stable-keeper; it only accepts pan dulce from Chuy.
- B) Tizón is given early (post-license or Hassaikai) as transport.
- C) It was already summoned in the Barranca fight; it's been with him since Mexico.
- D) No literal horse — only the cape's charro-silhouette (Ultimate 3).

**⭐ Justification for A:** a flying war-pegasus in Volume I erases the mobility tension your whole underdog design needs; late-earned it's the reward of the entire father arc. The folklore inversion is the point — the legend says you mount and can NEVER dismount (soul stolen); Chuy mounts and dismounts by choice every time, and gives rescue rides to civilians: the ride that stole souls becomes the ride that saves them. Naming it in the descent = ownership transferred aloud, matching the reclaimed face. Tizón beats Cenizo for energy and action-figure-ness, but this is genuinely your taste call.

### Q15. How does possession (the Grito / El Último Mariachi) escalate?
- **A) ⭐ (recommended bundle):** Grito borrows 10% with total amnesia ("What did I say?"), the Charro speaking old Spanish through him; each use deepens the borrow percentage and the voice gets faster/less polite. The first uncommanded borrow is the book's darkest chapter (the cape tries to claim mid-arrest AND the Charro takes seconds Chuy didn't grant). One payoff: after a Grito, witnesses report he spoke impossibly personal words of comfort to a classmate — the messenger channeling more than threats.
- B) The Charro can possess Chuy whenever he wants, no fight.
- C) No possession at all — the voice stays in dreams.
- D) Full, voluntary, stable merge by the finale (two beings in harmony).

**⭐ Justification for A:** consent is the novel's entire spine (the contract was signed without informed consent; his whole family is built on consent lessons); possession must therefore be the scariest thing in the book specifically when it happens WITHOUT permission. D as the ending removes the horror rather than defeating it — I'd instead let harmony arrive through the Charro's RELEASE, never Chuy's absorption.

# PART IV — THE LEDGER

### Q16. What does a "claimed" soul actually do to the living body?
- **A) ⭐ The lien model:** the body lives; the Quirk returns after 24 h; the memory of greed is erased; the soul is MARKED and collected at natural death (a debt on the books). Marked villains walk free from custody paranoid, haunted, terrified of a 16-year-old — Mendoza can return as a recurring figure. Add: a marked soul that genuinely atones before death VOIDS the lien (mercy exists even post-verdict).
- B) Instant extraction — body left vegetative/empty forever.
- C) Soul destroyed, outright.
- D) Escrow: the claim is fully reversible by atonement at any time.

**⭐ Justification for A:** it matches canon-on-page (Mendoza alive, 24 h Quirkless, hollowed), gives you a recurring rogue's gallery instead of one-scene trophies, keeps Chuy's hands literally clean of killing (he delivers, never executes — critical for his ethics AND Wattpad's content rules), and the atonement-void clause means his second-chances belief can win even AFTER the cape's verdict — proof delivered to the tribunal. B/C drift him toward anti-hero and poison the thesis.

### Q17. Is the quota structurally impossible in Japan?
- **A) ⭐ Yes — the jurisdictional engine:** Japan's canon villains run on grief, ideology, revenge, love, and broken systems (Shigaraki/Dabi/Toga/Stain/Spade of the League read clean or grey); true for-profit black stains are RARE (Re-Destro's corporatists, Slidin' Go). Mictlán's warrant doesn't run over Japanese soil — a different underworld (Yomi/Izanami) holds domain; the cape feels far from home, strongest on Mexican ground (consulate ofrenda; later the Sierra threshold). The stalled quota is what forces the nine-level descent.
- B) No — there are plenty of greed villains; the quota fills naturally.
- C) Mictlán is universal — geography is irrelevant to death gods.
- D) The Charro re-routes Chuy to hunt global cartel targets via intelligence feeds.

**⭐ Justification for A:** this is the idea that turns a fetch-quest into your novel: the contract was drafted in a country whose evil is GREED (cartels, Xolocristal) and served on a country whose evil is WOUNDS. A quota that CANNOT be fulfilled is a contract that is fundamentally fraudulent — Ana Sofía's malpractice case against the afterlife practically writes itself. It also protects every canon fan-favorite villain from your protagonist's kill-list and leaves Deku's villains intact until the handoff.

  - **#1 Mendoza** & **#2 El Alquimista** (locked, Mexico, lien model)

  - **#4 Re-Destro, #5 Trumpet, #6 Skeptic** — corporate/political greed, collected or marked during/after the PLW (Skeptic survives canon into the S7 chaos — timeline-friendly)
  - Then the ledger STALLS at 6/10. Curious gets honorable mention: already dead before Japan (Toga's hands; cape line: "Another collector reached her first").

**⭐ Justification for A:** six claims and a wall is mathematically purposeful — the quota fails on purpose, and every name on the list is a greed-soul under your own taxonomy while every beloved wound-villain survives to be SAVED. Overhaul-refused is the single most important "no" in the book: the most claimable man in the Hassaikai gets mercy after what he did to Eri, and mercy not costing Chuy nothing would be a lie. Slidin' Go's deathbed claim shows the cape working at its most terrifying precisely when Chuy can't stop it.

### Q19. What happens with Toga? [related to Q27]
- **A) ⭐ Failed claim → defection → LIFE, co-led with Uraraka:** the cape reads ZERO stain on the girl the whole world calls a monster — Chuy's pivotal theological crisis; he protects her instead of collecting; Uraraka remains the architect of her salvation (the canon transfusion scene, now shielded by Chuy's dome — he enables, doesn't replace). She lives, hides, flirts with him shamelessly, steals his blood a escondidas for pranks; her crush is real-ish (Toga loves what she wants to become) and answered once, kindly, as family. Shigaraki personally notices Skull King for taking one of his.
- B) Full redemption-girlfriend arc (see Q27-B).
- C) She still dies canonically; Chuy only witnesses.
- D) He claims her (the darkest possible Chuy — recommended against with both hands).

**⭐ Justification for A:** it delivers EVERY structural gift from your pitch (Shigaraki's interest, a rewritten canon death proving it's his story now, the "who decides who's saved" thesis with a face) while avoiding the two traps I flagged: replacing Uraraka's canon-defining friendship with boy-saves-killer romance, and the tonal/Wattpad-safety problem of dating an un-judged serial killer. Family-love Toga is also a character you can keep FUNNY through Act 3, which romantic Toga can't.

### Q20. How does the contract end, and what is the Charro's fate? (the tenth soul)
- **A) ⭐ The Charro is delivered as the tenth judgment — as a WITNESS, not a prisoner:** Chuy completes nine mortal verdicts HIS way (some claimed, some mercied — the tribunal must rule whether mercies count as valid "deliveries"), then names the entity himself: a debt officer who for centuries collected souls with no due process, no consent, and an endless chain of child replacements. Ana Sofía argues it before Mictlantecuhtli and Mictecacíhuatl using the "Contrato — Pruebas" file (minority, duress, undisclosed terms, never-accepted ultimatum, impossibility abroad). The office is abolished; the Charro — a tired man who once made a bargain and was never allowed to finish dying — is granted rest; the horse chooses Chuy free of lien.
- B) Chuy simply refuses and fights the Charro, tearing up the contract by force.
- C) Chuy becomes the next Charro and then escapes the office from inside.
- D) He renegotiates: stays employed as a heroic, voluntary collector.

**⭐ Justification for A:** it is the ONLY ending that pays off every planted gun simultaneously — the lawyer sister, the evidence notebook, the messenger-not-collector reframe, the fading greys, the impossible quota, the name ("Names don't matter where I come from. Only debts" → Chuy learns his NAME and releases him), and your mother's lesson (control over being seen) applied to a monster nobody truly saw for centuries. It also keeps the ending merciful: Chuy doesn't kill his abuser — he retires him. B is a shonen punch ending that wastes the legal family you built; C is a sequel hook this book doesn't need; D quietly vindicates the system instead of reforming it.

# PART VI — THE HANDOFF, THE HEART, THE HOMECOMING

### Q26. How does Deku step aside, and how is the finale split? [LOCKED-DIRECTION — confirm the method]
- **A) ⭐ Three-judgment finale:** Deku keeps **AFO** (the black-hole soul outside Mictlán's warrant — the bargainer who steals identities, Japan's own Charro Negro) at full power, glorious and conclusive.
